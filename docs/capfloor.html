Part 6 · Vanilla options

Caps & floors

A string of options on interest rate fixings, one per period. Price one option on one fixing, then add them up.

Level 1 The idea

A company borrows at a floating rate: every three months it pays whatever 3-month EURIBOR is, plus a margin. If rates jump, its interest bill jumps too. A cap fixes that. Every quarter, if EURIBOR is above the cap rate (the strike), the cap pays the difference. The company's rate can never go above the strike.

A floor is the mirror image. Every quarter, if EURIBOR is below the strike, the floor pays the difference. Lenders and investors in floating-rate notes buy floors so their income never drops below a minimum.

Each single-period option is a caplet or a floorlet. A one-year floor on 3-month EURIBOR is four floorlets in a row.

A thermostat with a minimum setting. Every quarter you check the temperature (the rate). If it is below 1.70°, the heater pays you for the gap. Each quarter is its own separate check, so four quarters means four chances to be paid.

Level 2 The cashflows

The buyer pays a premium today. Then, for each period, the rate fixes at the start and the floorlet may pay at the end of the period:

One period, paid at the period end Caplet = N × τ × max(L − K, 0)     Floorlet = N × τ × max(K − L, 0)

Level 3 The PV formula

Step 1 · Forward rate for each period

Each fixing Li is unknown. Its centre is the forward rate from the EURIBOR projection curve, exactly as for an FRA:

Fi = 1τi ( Pproj(si)Pproj(ei) − 1 )

Step 2 · One option on one rate: Bachelier

A caplet is a call on the rate, a floorlet a put. Rate desks model the rate with a bell curve measured in basis points: the normal (Bachelier) model. The normal vol σN is the typical yearly move in bp. 85bp means a typical move of ±0.85% in a year.

di = Fi − KσN√Ti
caplet rate value = (Fi − K) N(di) + σN√Ti n(di)
floorlet rate value = (K − Fi) N(−di) + σN√Ti n(di)
Ti
time to the fixing date of period i (option expiry), in years
n(d)
height of the bell curve: e−d²/2 ÷ √(2π) (see the toolkit)

Step 3 · Scale, discount and sum

The full cap or floor PV = Σi N × τi × D(ei) × Bachelier(Fi, K, Ti, σN,i)
τi
accrual of period i, ACT/360 for EURIBOR
D(ei)
OIS (€STR) discount factor to the payment date ei
σN,i
caplet vol for that expiry and strike. It can differ for each period

Cap − floor = swap. Long a cap and short a floor at the same strike pays L − K every period, whatever happens. That is a payer swap at rate K. Use it to check your prices, and to see that an ATM cap and floor are worth the same.

Level 4 Desk reality

  • Reading the trade description. Floor 1Y EUR-EURIBOR-3M 1.7 % 16/Mar/30 means: floor, 1-year tenor, on 3-month EURIBOR, strike 1.70%, ending 16 Mar 2030. We read it as four quarterly floorlets from 16 Mar 2029. Check the schedule on the trade itself.
  • Fixing lag. EURIBOR fixes two business days before each period starts. The option expiry Ti is that fixing date. We ignore the two days in the example.
  • Spot-starting caps skip the first period. Its rate is already fixed today, so there is nothing optional left.
  • Flat vol vs caplet vol. Brokers quote a cap with one "flat" vol for all its caplets. The desk strips these into a separate vol per caplet expiry, then interpolates by strike along the smile. Price with caplet vols.
  • Normal or shifted lognormal. Since negative rates, EUR desks quote normal vols in bp. Some systems use a shifted Black model instead: Black on F + shift and K + shift.
  • SOFR and €STR caps. Overnight-rate caps pay on a rate compounded over the period. It is only known at the end of the period, so the option keeps running through the accrual period. That adds a little extra time value.

Greeks that matter

GreekWhat it measuresFor a cap or floor
Delta (DV01)PV change if the forward rates rise 1bpPositive for a cap, negative for a floor. The example floor loses about EUR 1,617 per bp. It is bucketed by period, like a strip of FRAs weighted by each N(d).
VegaPV change for +1bp of normal volAlways positive for the buyer: EUR 2,856 per bp here. Bucketed by caplet expiry: it is the main risk a cap/floor desk runs.
GammaHow DV01 changes as rates movePositive for the buyer, largest for caplets near the money and close to fixing.
ThetaPV change from one day passingNegative for the buyer. Each floorlet decays fastest just before its own fixing.

Worked example

Valuation date 23 Nov 2026. A long EUR 50m floor on 3M EURIBOR, strike 1.70%, four quarterly periods from 16 Mar 2029 to 16 Mar 2030. Illustrative flat curves, continuously compounded ACT/365: EURIBOR zero 2.30%, €STR zero 2.10%. Normal vol 85bp for every floorlet.

  1. Floorlet 1 dates. Fixing 16 Mar 2029 (844 days), payment 16 Jun 2029 (936 days), period 92 days.
    T1 = 844/365 = 2.31233 e1 = 936/365 = 2.56438 τ1 = 92/360 = 0.25556
  2. Forward EURIBOR.
    P(2.31233) = e^(−0.023×2.31233) = 0.948206 P(2.56438) = 0.942725 F1 = (0.948206 / 0.942725 − 1) / 0.25556 = 2.2751%
  3. Bachelier inputs.
    σN√T = 0.0085 × √2.31233 = 0.0085 × 1.52063 = 0.012925 d = (0.022751 − 0.017) / 0.012925 = 0.44492 N(−d) = 0.328187 n(d) = 0.361347
  4. Floorlet rate value.
    (0.017 − 0.022751) × 0.328187 + 0.012925 × 0.361347 = −0.0018873 + 0.0046705 = 0.0027832
  5. Scale and discount.
    D(e1) = e^(−0.021×2.56438) = 0.947572 PV1 = 50,000,000 × 0.25556 × 0.947572 × 0.0027832 = 12,107,868 × 0.0027832 = EUR 33,699
  6. Repeat for the other three floorlets. They are worth more because they have longer to expiry.
    PV2 = 36,522 PV3 = 38,776 PV4 = 40,814

The floor is worth EUR 149,811 (about 30bp of notional). Every floorlet is out of the money today (forward 2.28% vs strike 1.70%), so the price is pure time value.

Remember

  • A cap is a strip of calls on each rate fixing, a floor a strip of puts.
  • For each period: forward from the projection curve, Bachelier with that caplet's normal vol, × N × τ × OIS discount factor.
  • Sum over periods. Cap − floor = payer swap at the strike.
  • The main risk is vega, bucketed by expiry. Delta is a weighted strip of FRA risks.
