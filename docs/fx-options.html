Part 6 · Vanilla options

FX vanilla options

The right to exchange one currency for another at a fixed rate. Every FX option is a call on one currency and a put on the other at the same time. Once that clicks, Garman-Kohlhagen is just Black-Scholes with two interest rates.

Level 1 The idea

An Indian exporter will receive US dollars next October and must convert them into rupees. If the rupee strengthens (USD:INR falls from 92.50 to 85), every dollar buys fewer rupees and the exporter loses money.

They buy a USD put / INR call struck at 90. It gives them the right to sell USD 10 million at 90 rupees per dollar on 1 Oct 2027. If USD:INR is below 90 then, they exercise and get INR 900 million. If it is above 90, they let the option expire and convert at the better market rate.

Swapping football stickers with a friend: "I give you my Messi, you give me two Ronaldos". The same deal is selling a Messi and buying Ronaldos. An FX option is always both: a USD put is also an INR call.

Pair notation. USD:INR = 92.50 means 1 US dollar costs 92.50 rupees. The first currency (USD) is the foreign or base currency, the thing being priced. The second (INR) is the domestic or quote currency, the unit of the price. Calls and puts in trade descriptions refer to the first currency.

Level 2 The cashflows

Today you pay a premium, settled on the spot date (two business days later). If you exercise at expiry, two currency amounts are swapped on the delivery date: you pay USD 10m and receive INR 900m. Both are contingent.

Level 3 The PV formula

Step 1 · The FX forward

Holding dollars earns the USD rate rf. Holding rupees earns the INR rate rd. The forward exchange rate makes the two equal (see FX forwards):

F = S × e(rd − rf)T

Higher INR rates make the forward dollar more expensive in rupees. That is why the 10-month forward (94.42) is well above spot (92.50).

Step 2 · Garman-Kohlhagen

Garman and Kohlhagen (1983) noticed that a foreign currency behaves like a share paying a dividend yield equal to the foreign rate. So take Black-Scholes and set r = rd and q = rf:

Price in domestic currency per 1 unit of foreign notional d1 = ln(F/K) + ½σ2Tσ√T    d2 = d1 − σ√T
Call = Se−rfT N(d1) − Ke−rdT N(d2)
Put = Ke−rdT N(−d2) − Se−rfT N(−d1)

Step 3 · Scale and convert

PVdomestic = Nforeign × price     PVforeign = PVdomestic ÷ S

The same option has four honest prices: INR pips, INR amount, USD amount and USD %. Before agreeing a price with a counterparty, agree the premium currency and the quoting convention. It is the most common source of FX option booking errors.

Level 4 Desk reality

The smile is quoted by delta

FX traders quote the smile at three points of the surface for each expiry:

  • ATM: the at-the-money vol (delta-neutral straddle).
  • 25-delta risk reversal (RR): vol of the 25-delta call minus vol of the 25-delta put. It shows which way the market fears a big move. For USD:INR it is usually positive: people pay more for protection against a rupee crash.
  • 25-delta butterfly (BF): how much higher both wings are than ATM. It shows how fat the tails are.
σ25C = σATM + BF + ½RR     σ25P = σATM + BF − ½RR

Example: ATM 5.5%, RR +1.2%, BF 0.3% gives σ25C = 6.4% and σ25P = 5.2%. To price your strike, you interpolate the vol at your option's delta.

Which delta?

  • Spot delta = −e−rfTN(−d1) for a put. This is the hedge in spot.
  • Forward delta = −N(−d1). This is the hedge in the forward. It is used for long-dated and EM pairs.
  • Premium-adjusted delta = spot delta − PVforeign %. Use it when the premium is paid in the foreign currency, because that premium is itself an FX exposure. USD:INR, USD:JPY and most EM pairs quote premium-adjusted.

Other things that bite

  • Dates. Expiry (10am New York or 3pm Tokyo cut) and delivery (expiry + spot lag) are different days. Vol runs to expiry. Discounting runs to delivery.
  • Non-deliverable currencies. Offshore USD:INR options are usually cash-settled in USD against the RBI reference rate, so no rupees change hands. The payoff in USD is N × max(K − ST, 0) ÷ ST.
  • Pegs. Call European 7.85 USD:HKD is struck at the weak edge of the Hong Kong dollar's 7.75–7.85 band. Normal vol is tiny, but the option is a bet on the peg breaking. A lognormal model says it is almost free, which is exactly the tail risk the buyer wants.
  • Two rate risks. You have rho to both the domestic and the foreign curve, and both must be hedged.

Worked example

A USD put / INR call on USD 10m, strike 90, expiring 1 Oct 2027 (312 days, T = 0.85479). Illustrative market data: spot 92.50, INR rate rd = 6.0%, USD rate rf = 3.6% (continuous), vol 6.0%.

  1. Forward.
    F = 92.50 × e^((0.06 − 0.036) × 0.85479) = 94.4172
  2. d1 and d2.
    σ√T = 0.06 × √0.85479 = 0.055473 d1 = (ln(94.4172/90) + ½ × 0.06² × 0.85479) / 0.055473 = (0.047914 + 0.001539) / 0.055473 = 0.89147 d2 = 0.89147 − 0.055473 = 0.83600
  3. Probability table.
    N(−d1) = N(−0.89147) = 0.186338 N(−d2) = N(−0.83600) = 0.201578
  4. Discounted strike and foreign-discounted spot.
    K e^(−rd T) = 90 × e^(−0.06×0.85479) = 85.50048 S e^(−rf T) = 92.50 × e^(−0.036×0.85479) = 89.69689
  5. Put price in INR per USD.
    P = 85.50048 × 0.201578 − 89.69689 × 0.186338 = 17.23500 − 16.71394 = 0.521056 INR
  6. Scale and convert.
    PV = 0.521056 × 10,000,000 = INR 5,210,560 = USD 56,330 (÷ 92.50) = 0.5633% of USD notional
  7. Deltas.
    spot −0.18069 · forward −0.18634 · premium-adjusted −0.18069 − 0.00563 = −0.18632

The exporter pays INR 5.21m (USD 56,330, 0.563% USD) for protection below 90. Their dollar hedge is to sell about USD 1.81m spot.

Try it

Defaults are the worked example. Try the HKD peg trade: spot 7.80, strike 7.85, call, 73 days, HKD rate 3.0%, USD rate 3.6%, vol 2%.

Remember

  • A call on the first currency is a put on the second. USD:INR = INR per 1 USD.
  • Garman-Kohlhagen = Black-Scholes with r = domestic rate and q = foreign rate.
  • The formula gives domestic currency per unit of foreign notional. Agree the premium currency before quoting.
  • The smile is quoted as ATM, 25-delta risk reversal and butterfly. Check which delta convention the pair uses.
  • Vega per vol point, delta in foreign currency, and two rhos.
