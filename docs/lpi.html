Part 6 · Vanilla options

LPI: capped & floored inflation

An inflation swap where each year's inflation is squeezed between 0% and 5% before it is added up. Under the hood it is a zero-coupon inflation swap plus a strip of small inflation options, one cap and one floor per year.

Level 1 The idea

Many UK pensions grow every year with inflation, but with two rules. If prices rise by more than 5% in a year, the pension only grows by 5%. If prices fall, the pension does not shrink: it grows by 0%. This rule is called Limited Price Indexation, or LPI.

A pension fund that has promised LPI pensions wants to receive exactly that kind of inflation. An LPI swap gives it that. One side pays the LPI-indexed growth of a notional, the other side pays a fixed growth rate agreed today.

Picture a thermostat that keeps a room between 0°C and 5°C above outside. Whatever the weather does, the reading you record each year is clipped into that band. LPI does the same thing to the yearly inflation number, then multiplies the clipped years together.

If you already know zero-coupon inflation swaps, you know 90% of this product. The new part is the clipping, and clipping is an option.

Level 2 The cashflows

Like a zero-coupon inflation swap, nothing is paid until maturity. Each year in between, an LPI rate is recorded:

One year's LPI rate Xi = min( max( yi , 0% ) , 5% )   with   yi = RPIiRPIi−1 − 1

At maturity T (in years), the two legs pay:

Payments at maturity Fixed leg: N × [ (1 + K)T − 1 ]     LPI leg: N × [ (1 + X1)(1 + X2)…(1 + XT) − 1 ]

Level 3 The PV formula

Step 1 · Split the clipped rate into pieces you can price

Clipping a number between 0% and 5% is the same as taking the number, removing the part above 5%, and adding back the part below 0%:

Exact identity (when floor < cap) min(max(y, 0%), 5%) = y − max(y − 5%, 0) + max(0% − y, 0)

Read it as: LPI rate = inflation − a cap at 5% + a floor at 0%. The cap and the floor are options on one year's inflation, called a year-on-year (YoY) caplet and floorlet.

Step 2 · Price each yearly caplet and floorlet with Bachelier

For year i, the market gives a forward YoY rate fi (what it expects that year's inflation to be) and a normal volatility σ (how many percentage points it might typically move per year). YoY inflation can be negative, so desks use the normal (Bachelier) model from the toolkit rather than Black:

Undiscounted YoY caplet (strike 5%) and floorlet (strike 0%), year i Capleti = (fi − 5%) N(dc) + σ√ti n(dc),   dc = fi − 5%σ√ti
Floorleti = (0% − fi) N(−df) + σ√ti n(df),   df = fi − 0%σ√ti
ti
years until year i's inflation is known (about i)
N(x)
the normal "probability table": chance a standard bell-curve number is below x
n(x)
the height of the bell curve at x: e−x²/2 ÷ √(2π)
Expected LPI rate for year i E[Xi] = fi − Capleti + Floorleti

Step 3 · Compound the years and discount

PV of the LPI leg (simplified) PVLPI = N × D(T) × [ ∏i = 1…T (1 + E[Xi]) − 1 ]
PV of the fixed leg, and of the swap for the LPI payer PVfixed = N × D(T) × [ (1 + K)T − 1 ]     PV = PVfixed − PVLPI
∏
"multiply together", the product version of Σ
D(T)
GBP discount factor to maturity (SONIA curve)

Step 3 replaces "expected value of a product" by "product of expected values". That is exact only if each year's inflation is independent of the others. In reality a high-inflation year tends to be followed by another, and the real LPI index is path-dependent. Treat this formula as a teaching approximation. Production models (for example Jarrow–Yildirim, or a YoY market model with year-to-year correlation) handle the dependence.

Level 4 Desk reality

  • Reading the trade description. Max(Min[GBP-LPI Mar-2M,5%], 0%) is the clipping rule. Mar-2M means the index is observed with a 2-month lag, anchored on March RPI. P on the LPI leg and R 3.421 % mean we pay LPI and receive 3.421% compounded over 20 years.
  • Who trades it. UK defined-benefit pension schemes are the natural receivers of LPI, because their liabilities are LPI-linked. Banks pay LPI to them and hedge with RPI zero-coupon swaps plus YoY caps and floors.
  • Smile. The 0% floor and the 5% cap sit far from the forward (around 3%), so each strike has its own normal vol. A single flat σ as in the calculator understates both options when the smile is steep.
  • The 0% floor is expensive when inflation is low. In 2020, with RPI near 1%, the floor was valuable. In 2022–23, with RPI above 10%, the 5% cap was deep in the money and LPI receivers got far less than RPI.
  • RPI reform. From February 2030 RPI is aligned with CPIH, which lowers expected RPI after 2030. This shows up as a drop in long-dated forward YoY rates.
  • Seasonality and lag. Monthly RPI has seasonal patterns. The 2-month lag means the first year's rate may be partly known already, which removes its option value.

Greeks that matter

Worked example

We pay LPI [0%, 5%] and receive fixed 3.00% on GBP 10m for 5 years. The forward YoY RPI is flat at 3.20%, the YoY normal vol is 1.20% (120bp) for every year, and the GBP discount rate is 4.00% continuously compounded.

  1. Year 1 caplet at 5%. Here σ√t = 1.20%.
    d_c = (3.20% − 5%) / 1.20% = −1.500 Caplet = (−1.80%)·N(−1.5) + 1.20%·n(1.5) = 0.03517%
  2. Year 1 floorlet at 0%.
    d_f = 3.20% / 1.20% = 2.667 Floorlet = (−3.20%)·N(−2.667) + 1.20%·n(2.667) = 0.00142%
  3. Year 1 expected LPI rate.
    E[X1] = 3.20% − 0.03517% + 0.00142% = 3.16625%
  4. Repeat for years 2 to 5. σ√t grows (1.70%, 2.08%, 2.40%, 2.68%), so both options grow.
    E[X2] = 3.09367% E[X3] = 3.03356% E[X4] = 2.98695% E[X5] = 2.95016%
  5. Compound the five years.
    1.0316625 × 1.0309367 × 1.0303356 × 1.0298695 × 1.0295016 = 1.1618701
  6. Fixed leg factor and discount factor.
    (1.03)^5 = 1.1592741 D(5) = e^(−0.04×5) = 0.818731
  7. Leg values.
    PV_LPI = 10,000,000 × 0.818731 × 0.1618701 = 1,325,280 PV_fixed = 10,000,000 × 0.818731 × 0.1592741 = 1,304,026
  8. Swap PV for the LPI payer.
    PV = 1,304,026 − 1,325,280 = −21,254

The swap is worth −GBP 21,254 to the LPI payer. Without the cap and floor, the RPI leg would be worth GBP 1,396,533. Clipping removes about GBP 71k of value, because the 5% cap removes more inflation than the 0% floor adds back.

Try it

Raise the vol and watch the expected LPI rates fall away from the forward. Move the forward towards 0% and watch the floor take over. The table shows each year's pieces.

Remember

  • LPI clips each year's inflation into [0%, 5%] and then compounds the years.
  • Clipped rate = inflation − YoY caplet at 5% + YoY floorlet at 0%.
  • Price each yearly option with Bachelier on the forward YoY rate, then multiply the expected yearly factors and discount.
  • Multiplying expectations ignores year-to-year correlation. It is a simplification, and the real product is path-dependent.
  • Risks: inflation01 (damped by the clipping), normal vega, DV01 and inflation correlation.
