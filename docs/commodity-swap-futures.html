Part 2 · Futures

Commodity swap & spread futures

A bet on the average oil price over a calendar month, cleared like a future. Refiners, airlines and shipping companies use them because they buy fuel every day, so the monthly average is the price they actually pay.

Level 1 The idea

An airline fills its planes every day. It does not care what jet fuel costs on one particular Tuesday. It cares what fuel costs on average this month. So it wants a hedge that pays the average.

A commodity swap future does exactly that. You agree a fixed price today, say $71.80 a barrel. At the end of the month, the exchange averages the published price over every pricing day. If the average is higher than your fixed price, you (the buyer) gain the difference on every barrel.

Think of your mobile data bill. You don't pay for the peak day, you pay for the month. An average-price swap is a fixed monthly plan: you lock the monthly price, and whoever is on the wrong side of the real average makes up the difference.

A spread future is the same idea on the difference between two averages, for example Singapore petrol minus Brent crude. That difference is a refiner's profit margin, called the crack spread.

Level 2 The cashflows

The contract settles once, after the month ends, against the monthly average A. Before that, like every future, it is margined daily against the exchange's settlement price. That price is the market's best guess of A.

Level 3 The PV formula

Step 1 · The settlement amount

Payoff at final settlement, long position (A − K) × Q × n,    A = 1D Σd = 1..D Pd
K
fixed (trade) price
Q
lot size: 1,000 bbl for Dubai and Brent swaps, 100 t for the mini fuel oil contract
D
number of pricing days in the month (days Platts publishes)
Pd
published price on pricing day d

Step 2 · Split the month into "known" and "still to come"

On any day inside the month, m prices are already published. The rest are unknown, so we replace each by its forward price Fd, as for every linear product.

Expected average E[A] = 1D ( Σpublished Pd + Σremaining Fd )

Step 3 · PV

Cleared (margined daily): no discounting PV = (E[A] − K) × Q × n

The OTC version of the same swap pays a few days after month end and has no daily margin. Then you multiply by the discount factor to the payment date: PVOTC = (E[A] − K) × Q × n × D(Tpay).

Spread futures (CommoditySpreadFuture)

Crack spread future, long the spread PV = ( E[Aproduct] − E[Acrude] − K ) × Q × n

Both legs must be in the same unit. Singapore Mogas 92 and Brent are both quoted in $/bbl, so you subtract directly. Fuel oil 380 is quoted in $/metric tonne. You first convert it with the industry factor 6.35 bbl per tonne: $455/t ÷ 6.35 = $71.65/bbl.

Level 4 Desk reality

  • What "1st line" means. On each pricing day the Brent 1st line price is the settlement of the front-month ICE Brent future that day. When the front month expires mid-month, the average silently rolls to the next contract. Your forward for the remaining days must follow the same roll.
  • Dubai is the Middle East crude benchmark priced by Platts. Dated Brent is physical North Sea cargoes loading in the next 10 days to one month. They are different prices with different forward curves.
  • Pricing calendars. Platts Singapore and London have different holidays. Count D from the right publication calendar. One wrong holiday shifts every weight by about 5%.
  • Delta melts through the month. Each published day removes 1/D of the risk. On the last pricing day almost nothing is left. Hedge ratios must be rebalanced daily.
  • Naming. Dubai 1st Line Swap Nov26 = November 2026 pricing month. SingM92U Vs Br1stL Dec26 = Singapore Mogas 92 unleaded minus Brent 1st line, December 2026 averages. FO Mini 380 Sing Swap (Mini) = fuel oil 380 cst Singapore, 100 t lots.

Worked example

On 23 Nov 2026 you hold 5 lots long Dubai 1st Line Swap Nov26 at $71.80. November 2026 has 21 pricing days. 15 are published (2 to 20 Nov) with an average of $72.40. The forward for the remaining 6 days is $73.10.

  1. Expected average.
    E[A] = (15 × 72.40 + 6 × 73.10) / 21 = (1,086.00 + 438.60) / 21 = $72.60
  2. PV.
    PV = (72.60 − 71.80) × 1,000 × 5 = $4,000
  3. Delta: only the 6 unpublished days can still move.
    Delta = 6/21 × 1,000 × 5 = $1,428.57 per $1 move in the forward

PV +$4,000 with delta $1,429 per $1/bbl, which is only 29% of the 5,000 bbl you started the month with.

Spread future

Long 4 lots SingM92U Vs Br1stL Dec26 at $10.80. December has not started, so both legs are fully forward. Mogas 92 December swap is $82.35/bbl and Brent 1st line December swap is $70.90/bbl.

  1. Fair crack.
    82.35 − 70.90 = $11.45/bbl
  2. PV.
    (11.45 − 10.80) × 1,000 × 4 = $2,600

Remember

  • The payoff is the monthly average price minus the fixed price, times barrels.
  • Expected average = published days at their real prices + remaining days at forward, divided by all pricing days.
  • Cleared contracts are not discounted; OTC versions are discounted to the payment date.
  • Spreads need both legs in the same unit (6.35 bbl per tonne for fuel oil).
  • Delta shrinks by 1/D every pricing day.
