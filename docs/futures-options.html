Part 6 · Vanilla options

Options on futures

Listed options where the underlying is a futures contract: crude oil, Nasdaq, Treasury notes, SONIA. One formula, Black-76, prices all four. Most of the work is reading the contract specification correctly.

Level 1 The idea

On the previous page the option was on a share or an index. Here the option is on a futures contract. If you exercise a call, you become long one future at the strike price. If you exercise a put, you become short one future at the strike.

The future is then immediately marked to the market price F. So exercising a put struck at 80 when crude futures trade at 76 credits you 80 − 76 = $4 per barrel through the margin account. Then you can close the future.

Imagine a voucher that lets you sign up for next season's football tickets at last year's price. The voucher does not give you a seat. It gives you a contract for a seat. If the new price is higher, the voucher is worth the difference.

Why trade options on futures instead of on the physical thing? Futures are where the liquidity is. Nobody wants to deliver 1,000 barrels of oil to settle an option. And the futures price already includes storage, interest and dividends, which makes pricing simpler.

Level 2 The cashflows

There are two styles of paying for the option. The style changes the cashflows and therefore the PV.

  • Premium-style (CME crude, Nasdaq and Treasury options): you pay the premium in cash the next day. At exercise you receive a futures position worth F − K (call) or K − F (put).
  • Futures-style (ICE SONIA and Euribor options): nobody pays a premium. The option is marked to market every day like a future, and gains and losses flow through variation margin. At expiry the total of all those flows equals payoff minus the trade price.

Level 3 The PV formula

Step 1 · The futures price is already the forward

For a share, Black-Scholes first had to build the forward: F = Se(r−q)T. For a futures option the market hands us F directly. Storage costs, interest and dividends are already inside it. The future costs nothing to enter, so there is no carry to adjust for.

Step 2 · Black-76

Fischer Black, 1976: Black-Scholes written on a forward d1 = ln(F / K) + ½σ2Tσ√T    d2 = d1 − σ√T
Call = D × [ F N(d1) − K N(d2) ]
Put = D × [ K N(−d2) − F N(−d1) ]
F
futures price for the contract the option is on
σ
implied lognormal vol for this strike and expiry
D
discount factor to premium payment: e−rT for premium-style, 1 for futures-style

Why D = 1 for futures-style? Daily margining means you never lend or borrow the premium. All the money moves day by day, so there is nothing to discount. Get this wrong and a long-dated SONIA option is mispriced by the interest on its premium.

Step 3 · The normal (Bachelier) version for interest-rate futures

A STIR future's price is 100 − rate. Rates can be close to zero, or even negative, so percentage moves make little sense. Rate desks assume the rate moves by a bell curve in basis points instead. That is the Bachelier model, with a normal vol σN in bp per year (see the toolkit):

d = F − KσN√T    Put = D × [ (K − F) N(−d) + σN√T × n(d) ]

n(d) is the height of the bell curve at d: e−d²/2 ÷ √(2π). Swap (K − F) for (F − K) and N(−d) for N(d) to get the call.

Step 4 · From price to money

PV = quantity × option price × point value

The point value is what a move of 1.00 in the quoted price is worth for one contract. It is specific to each contract. The next level lists it for every flavour.

The price is 100 − rate, so a put on the price is a call on the rate. The 94.8 put pays if the SONIA rate for that period ends above 5.20%. ICE 3M SONIA is £500,000 for 3 months, so 0.01 of price (1bp) = £12.50 and 1.00 point = £1,250. These options are futures-style: D = 1.

Level 5 Desk reality

  • Which future? Each option is written on one specific futures month, which is often not the nearest one. Crude weeklies use the front-month future. SONIA mid-curves use a contract a year or more away. Always price off the right F.
  • American on futures. Most CME futures options are American. Early exercise of a premium-style option is worth a little only when it is deep in the money and rates are high. Desks often use Black-76 and add a small tree-based adjustment. Futures-style options are never worth exercising early.
  • The smile. Crude puts trade at higher vols than calls when markets fear a crash, and the reverse during supply scares. Take σ for your strike from the vol surface.
  • Normal or lognormal. STIR and bond desks quote normal vol in bp (Bachelier). Commodity and equity desks quote lognormal vol in % (Black-76). The calculator lets you choose.
  • Expiry day. Weekly options expire in days, so theta and gamma are huge. A weekly can lose most of its value over a weekend.

Worked example

A weekly WTI put. Strike $80, crude future F = $79.10, 7 days to expiry (T = 7/365 = 0.019178), implied vol 36%, USD rate 3.6%, premium-style, 1,000 barrels per contract.

  1. Volatility over the life of the option.
    σ√T = 0.36 × √0.019178 = 0.049855
  2. d1 and d2.
    d1 = (ln(79.10/80) + ½ × 0.36² × 0.019178) / 0.049855 = (−0.011314 + 0.001243) / 0.049855 = −0.20201 d2 = −0.20201 − 0.049855 = −0.25186
  3. Probability table.
    N(−d1) = N(0.20201) = 0.58004 N(−d2) = N(0.25186) = 0.59943
  4. Inside the brackets.
    K N(−d2) − F N(−d1) = 80 × 0.59943 − 79.10 × 0.58004 = 47.9541 − 45.8815 = 2.0726
  5. Discount (premium-style).
    D = e^(−0.036 × 0.019178) = 0.999310 Put = 0.999310 × 2.0726 = $2.0711 per barrel
  6. Per contract and greeks.
    PV = 2.0711 × 1,000 = $2,071.14 delta −0.580 (short 0.58 futures) · vega $42.79 per vol point · theta −$110 per day

One weekly crude 80 put is worth $2.07/bbl = $2,071. Intrinsic value is $0.90, so $1.17 of the price is time value, and the option will lose roughly $110 of it each day.

Remember

  • Exercising a futures option gives you a futures position at the strike, worth F − K or K − F.
  • Black-76 is Black-Scholes with the futures price as the forward and no carry.
  • Premium-style: discount with e−rT. Futures-style: D = 1.
  • Rate futures use Bachelier and normal vol. A put on price is a call on the rate.
  • Read the contract: 103-24 = 103.75, premiums in 64ths, point values $1,000, $20 and £1,250.
