Part 7 · Exotics & volatility

Variance & volatility swaps

A swap where one leg is how much the market actually moved. You agree a volatility today. At maturity you measure the real volatility from daily prices and settle the difference. No strikes, no delta hedging: a pure bet on movement.

Level 1 The idea

Options let you bet on volatility, but they come with baggage. Their value also depends on where the price goes, so you must keep hedging the direction (delta). A volatility swap removes the baggage. You agree a number today, say 5.975% for NZD/SGD. At maturity, someone computes how volatile NZD/SGD really was from its daily closing prices. If it was 7%, you win 7 − 5.975 = 1.025 "vol points" times your notional per point.

A variance swap is the same bet on vol squared. Squaring sounds odd, but variance is much easier to price and hedge with options, so variance swaps are the standard in equity indices like the Nikkei 225. Volatility swaps are common in FX.

It is like betting on how bumpy a bus ride will be rather than on where the bus ends up. You fit a bump counter to the bus, and at the end of the trip you are paid for every bump above the number you agreed.

Level 2 The cashflows

Nothing is paid at the start. Every trading day a closing price is recorded. At maturity, one net payment is made:

Realised volatility, annualised, in vol points σreal = 100 × √( 252n Σi = 1…n [ ln( SiSi−1 ) ]² )
Payments at maturity, to the buyer Variance swap: Nvar × ( σreal² − Kvar² )
Volatility swap: Nvega × ( σreal − Kvol )
n
number of daily returns in the period; 252 = trading days per year
Nvega
money per vol point, e.g. NZD 100,000 per point
Nvar
money per variance point; market convention Nvar = Nvega ÷ (2Kvar)

Level 3 The PV formula

Step 1 · Why Nvar = Nvega ÷ 2K

Near the strike, σ² − K² = (σ − K)(σ + K) ≈ 2K(σ − K). Dividing by 2K makes a small move of 1 vol point worth about Nvega, the same as a vol swap. Traders talk in vega notional and let the system convert.

Step 2 · Mid-life: mix what happened with what is expected

Variance adds up day by day. After ne of the n days have passed, the final realised variance is a weighted average of the part already realised and the part still to come. The market's expectation for the remaining days is the implied variance for that period:

Expected final variance E[σreal²] = ne × σe² + (n − ne) × σimp²n
Variance swap PV for the buyer PVvar = D(T) × Nvar × ( E[σreal²] − Kvar² )
σe
realised vol over the elapsed days, from the formula above
σimp
the variance-swap level the market quotes for the remaining period

Step 3 · Where the fair variance strike comes from

A remarkable result: a portfolio of out-of-the-money puts and calls across all strikes, each held in quantity proportional to 1/K², behaves exactly like variance. Its value is the fair variance strike. This is also how the VIX index is computed:

Fair variance strike from option prices (VIX-style) Kvar² ≈ 2T · D(T) Σstrikes ΔKK² × OTM option price(K)

Low strikes get the biggest weight (1/K² is large there). Equity indices have a skew, where low-strike puts trade at high vols, so the variance strike usually sits a few points above the at-the-money vol. A Nikkei variance swap at 29.7 when ATM vol is around 25 is typical.

Step 4 · Volatility swaps and the convexity adjustment

Vol is the square root of variance. A square root bends downward, so the average of √(variance) is less than √(average variance). The gap grows with how uncertain variance is. If ν is the relative uncertainty of realised variance (its standard deviation divided by its average):

Fair volatility swap strike (second-order approximation) Kvolfair ≈ √Kvar − Var(σ²)8 Kvar3/2 = √Kvar × ( 1 − ν²8 )
Volatility swap PV for the buyer PVvol = D(T) × Nvega × ( Kvolfair − Kvol )

Here √Kvar means the variance-swap level quoted in vol points (6.40 in the example, so Kvar = 6.40²).

Level 4 Desk reality

  • Reading the trade description. EquityIndexVarSwap JPY NIKKEI 225 29.7 18/Mar/27 is a JPY variance swap on the Nikkei 225 struck at 29.7 vol, maturing 18 Mar 2027. FxVolSwap NZD NZD:SGD 5.975 15/Feb/27 is an FX vol swap, notional in NZD, strike 5.975 vol.
  • Caps. Since 2008 index variance swaps are usually capped at 2.5 × strike (in vol terms), because a crash makes variance explode. The cap is a short option on variance and slightly lowers the fair strike.
  • Mean. The standard formula does not subtract the average return. With daily data the difference is negligible.
  • Market disruption. Missing fixings (holidays, exchange outages) are handled by the term sheet: n counts actual observations, 252 stays fixed.
  • Vol swaps need a model. The convexity adjustment depends on vol of vol, which must come from a stochastic-vol model (Heston, SABR) calibrated to the smile. The ν²/8 shortcut gives the right size.
  • Daily P&L. Each day the buyer gains if that day's squared return × 252 beats the strike squared. A quiet day is a small loss, a big move a gain, with no delta hedging needed.

Worked example

A · Nikkei variance swap, mid-life

Long JPY 10m vega notional, strike 29.7, maturing 18 Mar 2027. The swap covers 124 trading days, and 46 have passed with realised vol 24.0. The market quotes 27.0 for the remaining 78 days. JPY rate 0.75%, 115 calendar days to payment.

  1. Variance notional.
    N_var = 10,000,000 / (2 × 29.7) = JPY 168,350 per variance point
  2. Expected final variance.
    E[σ²] = (46 × 24.0² + 78 × 27.0²) / 124 = (26,496 + 56,862) / 124 = 672.24 (√ = 25.93 vol)
  3. Compare with the strike squared, then discount.
    K² = 29.7² = 882.09 D = e^(−0.0075 × 115/365) = 0.997640
  4. PV.
    PV = 0.997640 × 168,350 × (672.24 − 882.09) = JPY −35,244,575

The long variance swap is worth −JPY 35.2m. Expected vol of 25.9 is 3.8 points below the strike, and 3.8 × 10m ≈ 38m (variance convexity softens the loss). One more point of implied vol on the remaining period would add about JPY 5.8m.

B · NZD/SGD volatility swap at inception

Long NZD 100,000 vega notional, strike 5.975, maturing 15 Feb 2027 (84 days). The variance swap for the same dates trades at 6.40. Our estimate of the relative uncertainty of realised variance is ν = 35%. NZD rate 3.0%.

  1. Convexity adjustment.
    6.40 × 0.35² / 8 = 0.098 vol points
  2. Fair vol strike.
    K_vol fair = 6.40 − 0.098 = 6.302
  3. Discount and PV.
    D = e^(−0.03 × 84/365) = 0.993120 PV = 0.993120 × 100,000 × (6.302 − 5.975) = NZD 32,475

The vol swap is worth +NZD 32,475 to the buyer. The fair vol strike of 6.302 is above the traded 5.975.

Remember

  • Realised vol = √(252/n × Σ daily log-return²), in vol points.
  • Var swap: N_var × (σ² − K²), with N_var = N_vega / 2K. Mid-life, mix realised and implied variance weighted by days.
  • The fair variance strike comes from a 1/K² strip of options, so skew pushes it above ATM vol.
  • Vol swap fair strike ≈ var strike × (1 − ν²/8). Variance is convex in vol, and that convexity has a price.
  • Main risk: vega, which shrinks as days are realised. No delta to manage.
