Part 3 · Swaps

Cross-currency swaps

Lend money in one currency and borrow the same value in another, then swap the interest back and forth until you hand the money back. Desks book three flavours: fixed notional, mark-to-market resetting, and non-deliverable.

Level 1 The idea

A Turkish company has US dollars but needs Turkish lira (TRY) for a year. A bank has the opposite need. They swap: today the company hands over USD 10m and receives TRY 420m (the spot rate is 42 TRY per dollar). Each quarter, each side pays interest on the money it received. After a year they swap the same amounts back.

Each side ends up borrowing one currency against the other as collateral. Nobody is exposed to the exchange rate on the notional, because the same amounts come back at the same fixed rate.

Two exchange students swap bikes for a year. Each pays the other a small rent each month for the bike they're riding, and at the end they swap back. The bikes are the notionals, and the rent is the interest on each leg.

The three product types differ in two details: whether the notional is fixed or resets to the FX rate, and whether the payments are actually delivered in both currencies.

Level 2 The cashflows

Fixed-notional swap (CurrencySwap)

You lend TRY and borrow USD: receive TRY fixed 37.55%, pay USD SOFR. There are three kinds of flow: the initial exchange, the coupons and the final exchange. Arrows are drawn in USD-equivalent size, so the notional exchanges dwarf the coupons.

Mark-to-market resetting swap (MTMCurrencySwap)

Over a long trade the FX rate can move a lot, and then one side owes the other a large amount on the final exchange. An MTM swap avoids this build-up. At every coupon date the USD notional is reset to (ZAR notional ÷ today's FX fixing), and the difference between the old and new USD notional is paid at once. The ZAR notional never changes.

Non-deliverable cross-currency swap (CurrencyNds)

The flows are the same as a fixed-notional swap, but the restricted-currency leg (KRW, TWD, INR) is never delivered. Each KRW amount, including any notional exchange, is converted to USD at the FX fixing a few days before payment and paid in USD. It works like the NDS with notional exchanges added.

Level 3 The PV formula

The rule is simple: price each leg in its own currency, on its own discount curve, then convert at today's spot.

Step 1 · each leg, in its own currency, including notionals PVleg = N × [ − D(t0) + Σi ci τi D(ti) + D(tn) ]
ci
coupon rate for period i: the fixed rate, or the forward floating rate Fi plus the spread
−D(t0)
the initial exchange: you hand this notional over. Drop it once it has happened.
+D(tn)
the notional coming back at maturity
Step 2 · convert the foreign leg at spot and add (S = foreign units per USD) PVUSD = PVUSD leg + PVforeign legS

A floating leg plus its notional, discounted on the same curve that projects its rate, is worth exactly par. For a USD SOFR leg discounted on SOFR OIS, −1 + Σ FiτiDi + Dn = 0. So a fresh USD SOFR leg is worth zero and all the value sits on the other leg.

Which foreign discount curve? The cross-currency basis

Trades are collateralised in USD, so foreign flows must be discounted on a curve that is consistent with FX forwards. That curve is read off the FX forward market:

Dforeign(t) = DUSD(t) × SXfwd(t)

This implied curve is generally not the foreign onshore OIS curve. The gap is the cross-currency basis: the extra spread one side pays to borrow a currency through FX markets. In a float-for-float swap it shows up as the quoted spread on the non-USD leg, like the −0.16% in the ZAR example.

MTM swap: why the USD leg drops out

Each period, the USD leg starts with a freshly reset notional, earns SOFR, and returns that notional. Every period is a little par loan, worth zero. Only the current period, whose notional is already set, carries value. So:

Float-for-float MTM swap, paying the foreign leg at spread s PVUSD ≈ Nforeign × (smkt − s) × AforeignS    with   Aforeign = Σ τi Dforeign(ti)

smkt is today's market basis spread for the remaining tenor. Exactly, smkt = Σ(FDi − Fi)τiDi ÷ A. Here FD is the forward from the discount curve and F the forward from the projection curve.

Non-deliverable: same answer, converted at fixings

PVUSD = PVUSD leg + Σi KRW flowiXfwd(ti) DUSD(ti) = PVUSD leg + PVKRW leg on NDF curveS

Level 4 Desk reality

  • Reading the trade description. USD P SOFRCMP-1D/TRY R 37.55 % means pay USD compounded SOFR, receive TRY fixed 37.55%. OISCMP-1D-0.16 % is the ZAR compounded overnight rate minus 16bp, and KSDA-3M-0.73 % is the KRW 3M CD rate minus 73bp.
  • Conventions. USD SOFR legs are ACT/360, compounded in arrears with a payment lag. Each foreign leg follows its own market (ZAR ACT/365, KRW ACT/365 quarterly). Notional exchanges settle on spot-date conventions.
  • FX risk on the notional. At inception, the initial and final exchanges nearly cancel. Once the initial exchange is done, you hold a foreign notional receivable and a USD payable, so the FX delta becomes large. MTM resets keep that exposure to one quarter's move.
  • Live trades. For a trade booked at an old spot, the notionals still use the old rate. The PV then includes the FX gain or loss on the final exchange.
  • Curves in practice. USD OIS, the foreign projection curve, and a foreign "discounted in USD" curve built from FX forwards and quoted basis swaps.

Worked example

Fixed notional: receive TRY fixed, pay USD SOFR

Today USD/TRY spot is 42.00. You trade a fresh 1-year swap: USD 10m against TRY 420m. You receive TRY 37.55% fixed quarterly (¼ year per period) and pay USD SOFR flat. The TRY curve implied by FX forwards (basis included) is a flat 35% continuously compounded. USD is discounted on SOFR OIS.

  1. USD leg: +10m now, then −SOFR coupons, then −10m at the end. By the key idea above, this is worth exactly zero.
    PV(USD leg) = 10m × (1 − 1) = 0
  2. TRY discount factors and annuity.
    D(0.25..1) = e^(−0.35 t) A = Σ 0.25 × D(t_i) = 0.807373 D(1) = 0.704688
  3. TRY leg: pay 420m now, receive four coupons of 420m × 37.55% × 0.25 = TRY 39,427,500, get 420m back.
    PV(TRY) = 420m × (−1 + 0.3755 × 0.807373 + 0.704688) = TRY 3,299,728
  4. Convert at spot.
    PV = 3,299,728 / 42.00 = USD 78,565
  5. Par fixed rate and risks.
    K_par = (1 − 0.704688) / 0.807373 = 36.577% Basis01 ≈ −USD 886 per bp FX delta (TRY 1% weaker) ≈ −USD 778

The swap is worth +USD 78,565 to you. You receive 37.55% where the market's fair rate is 36.58%.

MTM float-for-float: pay ZAR OIS − 0.16%, receive USD SOFR

  1. ZAR 180m (USD 10m at spot 18.00), 3 years quarterly. Illustrative curves: ZAR OIS projection 7.00% and ZAR-in-USD discount 7.10%, both continuously compounded.
    quarterly forwards: F = 7.0616% F^D = 7.1634% A = 2.678117
  2. Market basis spread, then PV.
    s_mkt = 7.1634% − 7.0616% = +0.1018% PV = 180m × (0.1018% − (−0.16%)) × 2.678117 / 18.00 = USD 70,107

Paying ZAR at −16bp when the market charges +10.2bp is worth +USD 70,107. Spread01 ≈ USD 2,678 per bp.

Remember

  • Price each leg in its own currency, with notionals, then convert at spot.
  • A floating leg plus notional on its own curve is worth par. That is why a USD SOFR leg is worth zero.
  • Foreign flows are discounted on the curve implied by FX forwards. Its gap to onshore OIS is the cross-currency basis.
  • MTM resets remove the FX build-up. Non-deliverable versions convert every local flow at an FX fixing.
