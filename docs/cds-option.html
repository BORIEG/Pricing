Part 6 · Vanilla options

CDS index options

The right, but not the obligation, to buy or sell protection on a CDS index at a spread fixed today. Credit traders use them to bet on, or hedge against, credit spreads blowing out.

Level 1 The idea

On the CDS index page we bought or sold protection at today's spread. An option lets you decide later. You pay a premium today for the right to enter the index trade on the expiry date at a strike spread K.

  • A payer option is the right to buy protection (pay the premium) at K. It pays off if spreads widen above K, so it is a call on the spread.
  • A receiver option is the right to sell protection (receive the premium) at K. It pays off if spreads tighten below K, so it is a put on the spread.

The example trade Sell Rec 225 16/Dec/26 iTraxx Europe Crossover Series 45 EUR 500 Jun 2031 means we sold a receiver struck at 225bp, expiring 16 Dec 2026. The underlying is the Crossover S45 index maturing June 2031, which has a 500bp coupon. We collected a premium. If Crossover trades tighter than 225bp at expiry, the buyer will exercise and we must sell them protection at 225bp, which is worse than the market.

A payer option is like buying a ticket that lets you buy flood insurance next month at today's price. If a storm forecast appears and insurance becomes expensive, your ticket is valuable. If the weather stays calm, you throw the ticket away and lose only what you paid for it.

Level 2 The cashflows

  1. Today: the option buyer pays the premium, an upfront amount quoted in bp or cents of notional.
  2. At expiry: the buyer exercises if it is worth it. They then enter the index trade at spread K, which settles as an upfront cash amount.
  3. After exercise: the normal index cash flows run until maturity, with premiums against protection on defaults.
  4. Defaults before expiry: an index option does not knock out. A payer holder who exercises also receives the losses on names that defaulted between trade and expiry. This is the front-end protection (FEP).

Level 3 The PV formula

Step 1 · The forward risky annuity

Once exercised, a trade at spread K versus a market spread S is worth (S − K) × RPV01 to the protection buyer. Seen from today, we need the RPV01 of a CDS starting at expiry Te, weighted by survival from today. That is the forward risky annuity A:

Forward risky annuity and forward spread A = Σti > Te τi D(ti) Q(ti−1) + Q(ti)2 F = (1 − R) Σ D(ti)[Q(ti−1) − Q(ti)]A

Step 2 · Add the front-end protection

Losses on defaults before expiry go to the payer holder on exercise. Their value today is (1 − R) × (chance of default before expiry) × D(Te). Spread over the annuity, it raises the forward:

Loss-adjusted forward spread FEP = (1 − R) D(Te) [1 − Q(Te)] F′ = F + FEPA

Step 3 · Black's formula on the spread

Treat the spread at expiry as lognormal with volatility σ, exactly as in Black-76, and use A in place of the discount factor:

Option premium per unit notional d1 = ln(F′/K) + ½σ2Teσ√Te d2 = d1 − σ√Te
Payer = A × [ F′ N(d1) − K N(d2) ]
Receiver = A × [ K N(−d2) − F′ N(−d1) ]
Te
time to expiry in years (ACT/365)
σ
lognormal spread volatility (Crossover often 40–60%)
Q, D
survival (flat hazard λ solved from the index spread) and OIS discount factors

Check: Payer − Receiver = A × (F′ − K), the value of a forward index trade at K including front-end protection. This put-call parity is the quickest test that your pricer is wired correctly.

Level 4 Desk reality

  • Fixed coupon at exercise. The index trades with a fixed 100bp or 500bp coupon, so exercise pays an upfront amount. The payer receives U(S) − U(K), where U(x) = (x − c) × RPV01(x) is computed with the standard flat-curve model at spread x. The strike's RPV01 is fixed by convention, not by the market. Dealers therefore use a slightly adjusted Black model (the Pedersen approach) that values this exact payoff. For a 3-week Crossover option the difference from Step 3 is small. For long-dated, far out-of-the-money options it matters.
  • Price-quoted strikes. CDX HY options are struck in price (for example 104.5), not spread. Same model, different strike units.
  • Expiries are monthly, usually on the third Wednesday (16 Dec 2026 here), and the options are European. Premium settles T+3. Most are quoted in cents upfront.
  • Vol surface. Desks quote implied volatility by strike and expiry. Payers further out of the money usually carry higher vol (skew), because spreads blow out quickly in a crisis.
  • Delta exchange. Options usually trade with an index hedge attached ("delta exchanged"), so the premium you see includes a hedge at a reference spread.

Worked example

Today is 23 Nov 2026. We have sold a EUR 10m receiver on iTraxx Crossover S45, strike 225bp, expiring 16 Dec 2026 (23 days). The underlying index matures 20 Jun 2031. The market has Crossover at 240bp, volatility 45%, R = 40% and EUR OIS 2.00%.

  1. Solve the flat hazard rate that prices the index at 240bp (as on the CDS page).
    λ = 4.0556% T_e = 23/365 = 0.063014
  2. Survival and discount to expiry.
    Q(T_e) = e^(−0.040556×0.063014) = 0.997448 D(T_e) = e^(−0.02×0.063014) = 0.998741
  3. Forward annuity from expiry to maturity (19 dates, first period 16 → 20 Dec), and the forward spread.
    A = 3.978022 forward protection = 0.095473 F = 0.095473 / 3.978022 = 240.00bp
  4. Front-end protection and adjusted forward.
    FEP = 0.6 × 0.998741 × (1 − 0.997448) = 0.0015295 F′ = 240.00bp + 0.0015295 / 3.978022 = 240.00 + 3.84 = 243.84bp
  5. Black's formula with σ√Te = 0.45 × 0.251025 = 0.112961.
    d1 = (ln(243.84/225) + ½ × 0.112961²) / 0.112961 = (0.080431 + 0.006380) / 0.112961 = 0.768506 d2 = 0.768506 − 0.112961 = 0.655544 N(−d1) = 0.221093 N(−d2) = 0.256059
  6. Receiver premium.
    Receiver = 3.978022 × (0.0225 × 0.256059 − 0.0243845 × 0.221093) = 0.0014722 → 14.72bp of notional PV seller = −10,000,000 × 0.0014722 = −EUR 14,722
  7. Parity check and Greeks.
    Payer = 0.0089686 Payer − Receiver = 0.0074965 = 3.978022 × (0.0243845 − 0.0225) ✓ delta = +EUR 880 per bp vega = −EUR 723 per vol pt theta = +EUR 707 per day

The receiver is worth 14.7bp of notional. Having sold it, your position is marked at −EUR 14,722. That is the premium you collected, which you keep if Crossover stays above 225bp.

Remember

  • Payer = call on the spread (buy protection at K). Receiver = put on the spread (sell protection at K).
  • Value = forward risky annuity × Black(F′, K, σ, Te), with the forward raised by front-end protection.
  • Parity: payer − receiver = A(F′ − K).
  • Delta is measured against index CS01. Sellers are short vega and gamma and collect theta. Compare with swaptions, which have the same structure with an interest-rate annuity.
