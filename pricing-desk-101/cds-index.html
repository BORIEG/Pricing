Part 4 · Credit

CDS indices

One contract that sells (or buys) protection on a whole basket of companies at once, in equal slices. It is the most liquid credit product in the world and the way the desk trades "credit" as a market.

Level 1 The idea

On the CDS page we insured one company. A CDS index insures 125 companies at once, each with the same weight. If you sell EUR 50m of protection on iTraxx Europe Main, you have effectively sold EUR 400,000 of protection on each of 125 large European companies.

You receive one premium on the whole basket. Each time one of the companies defaults, you pay its loss on its slice. That slice is then removed and the rest of the contract carries on.

Think of a football sticker album where you insure every sticker in the pack, not just one player. Losing one sticker costs you a little and you keep going with the rest. The premium is set by how fragile the whole pack is on average.

Level 2 The cashflows

The legs are the same as a single-name CDS, scaled by the basket.

  • Premium leg. The fixed coupon × outstanding notional × τ is paid on each IMM date (20 Mar, 20 Jun, 20 Sep, 20 Dec).
  • Protection leg. When one of the n names defaults, the seller pays (1 − Rname) × N ÷ n. The outstanding notional falls by N ÷ n.

The index factor tracks how much notional survives: f = (names left) ÷ (original names). The version in the name counts defaults. Series 34 Version 1 has had no defaults yet. After the first one, trades move to Version 2 with a factor below 1.

Level 3 The PV formula

Step 1 · An index is an average of single-name CDS

Each name k has its own hazard rate λk. The exact value of the index is the average of the n single-name contracts, all with the index coupon c:

Intrinsic value (per unit of current notional) PVseller = 1n Σk [ c × RPV01k − Protk ]

This sum is called the intrinsic value. Notice that we average values, not spreads. Wide names have a smaller RPV01 because they are likely to drop out early, so the average spread overstates the index spread a little.

Step 2 · The market shortcut: one flat curve

In practice the index is quoted as a single spread S. Everyone converts it to cash by pretending the index is one company with a flat hazard rate. That is the single-name model from the CDS page, run once:

Index quote to cash solve λ so that Prot(λ) ÷ RPV01(λ) = S
upfront paid by buyer = Prot − c × RPV01
price = 100 × (1 − upfront)
PVseller = f × N × ( c × RPV01 − Prot )
N, f
original notional and index factor (outstanding notional = f × N)
S
the quoted index spread
c
the index's fixed coupon (100bp or 500bp)

Step 3 · Price quotes (CDX HY)

High-yield indices are quoted as a price, like a bond: price = 100 − upfront in points. A price of 106.5 on a 500bp index means the buyer of protection receives 6.5 points, because the 500bp coupon is more than the risk is worth. To get the spread, solve the flat-curve model backwards for the S that gives that price. The calculator below does both directions.

The quoted spread is a convention for turning the price into cash. The difference between the quote and the intrinsic value is the index basis, typically a few bp. Arbitrage desks trade the index against its 125 single names when the basis gets large.

Level 4 Desk reality

  • Rolls. A new series launches every 20 March and 20 September with refreshed constituents and a 5-year maturity (for example Series 46 matures 20 Dec 2031). Most liquidity sits in the newest "on-the-run" series. Older ones such as iTraxx Japan Series 34 become "off-the-run".
  • Defaults. The defaulted name settles through the ISDA auction at its own recovery. A new version starts trading with factor (n − 1) ÷ n. The trade description keeps the series and version, and the notional shown is usually the original amount, so always multiply by the factor.
  • Upfront settles T+3 business days, with accrued since the last IMM date exchanged as for single names.
  • Clearing. Index trades are cleared at ICE Clear Credit / ICE Clear Europe, margined daily, and discounted on OIS.
  • Japan. JPY 100 in the trade description is the 100bp coupon, not the notional.

Worked example

Today is 23 Nov 2026. We sell EUR 50m of protection on iTraxx Europe Main Series 46 (125 names, 100bp coupon, maturity 20 Dec 2031, no defaults so far, f = 1). The quote is 58bp. We use R = 40% and EUR OIS at 2.00% flat.

  1. There are 21 quarterly IMM dates from 20 Dec 2026 to 20 Dec 2031. Solve the flat hazard rate that prices to 58bp. The credit triangle guess is 0.0058 ÷ 0.6 = 0.97%.
    λ = 0.98009%
  2. Value the two legs per unit notional, exactly as on the CDS page.
    RPV01 = 4.764964 Prot = 0.027637
  3. Upfront and price. The seller receives 100bp but the risk is only worth 58bp, so the seller pays.
    upfront (buyer pays) = 0.027637 − 0.01 × 4.764964 = −0.020013 → −2.0013 points price = 100 − (−2.0013) = 102.0013
  4. PV of our short-protection position (equal to what we paid upfront at this quote).
    PV = 1 × 50,000,000 × (0.01 × 4.764964 − 0.027637) = +EUR 1,000,642
  5. Risks.
    CS01 = −EUR 24,233 per bp JTD per name = −50,000,000/125 × 0.6 − 1,000,642/125 = −240,000 − 8,005 = −EUR 248,005
  6. A default in Crossover (75 names) on EUR 10m with recovery 30%:
    seller pays 10,000,000/75 × 0.70 = EUR 93,333 new factor = 74/75 = 0.98667 notional → EUR 9,866,667

At 58bp the index trades at a price of 102.00. Our EUR 50m short-protection position is worth +EUR 1,000,642 and loses about EUR 24,233 for each 1bp of widening.

Remember

  • An index is an equal-weight basket of single-name CDS with one fixed coupon.
  • Quote to cash: one flat hazard rate, upfront = Prot − c × RPV01, price = 100 − upfront.
  • Defaults pay (1 − R)/n per name and shrink the notional by the factor (n − 1)/n.
  • CS01 for daily risk, JTD per name for stress. Next: slicing the basket's losses into tranches.
