Part 2 · Futures

Bond futures

A future where the seller delivers a government bond of their choice from a basket. Pricing it means finding the bond the seller will pick, the cheapest-to-deliver, and working out what it costs to hold that bond until delivery.

Level 1 The idea

Suppose you buy a future that says "in March you will receive a US Treasury bond with 15 to 25 years left". Many bonds qualify, each with a different coupon. The seller chooses which one to hand over, so they will pick whichever is cheapest for them. That bond is the cheapest-to-deliver, or CTD.

To make different bonds comparable, the exchange gives each one a conversion factor (CF), roughly its price if all yields were 6%, per 1 of face value. A high-coupon bond is worth more, so it has a bigger CF and the buyer pays more for it.

A shop promises to deliver "a bag of apples, any variety". Each variety has a fixed exchange rate: one bag of Gala counts as 0.85 of the standard bag. The shop will deliver whichever variety is cheapest after applying those rates. To price the promise you price that one variety.

Level 2 The cashflows

Day to day a bond future is margined like any future: $1,000 per point per contract (face value $100,000), with prices quoted in 32nds. 118-24 means 118 + 24/32 = 118.75.

At delivery the seller hands over $100,000 face of the chosen bond. The buyer pays the invoice price:

Invoice amount per 100 face Invoice = F × CF + AIdel

To see why the futures price is what it is, look at the trade arbitrageurs run, the cash-and-carry. Buy the CTD today, collect its coupon, and deliver it into the future.

Level 3 The PV formula

Step 1 · Forward price of the CTD

Holding the bond to delivery costs repo interest and earns the coupon. The forward dirty price is what you must get back at delivery to break even:

Forward dirty price at delivery (repo is simple, ACT/360) Bfwd = (P + AI0)(1 + rrepo d360) − c (1 + rrepo dc360)
P, AI0
clean price and accrued interest of the CTD today
d
days from today's settlement to delivery
c, dc
coupon paid before delivery and days from that coupon to delivery (reinvested at repo)

Step 2 · Fair futures price

Invoice must equal the forward price Ffair = Bfwd − AIdelCF

The market future trades slightly below this, because the seller owns valuable choices (which bond, which day). That gap is the delivery option value.

Step 3 · Basis and implied repo, the trader's dashboard

Three numbers every bond-futures trader watches Gross basis = P − F × CF
Net basis = Gross basis − carry,   carry = P − (Bfwd − AIdel)
Implied repo = ( F×CF + AIdel + c(1 + rrepodc/360)P + AI0 − 1 ) × 360d

The CTD is the bond with the highest implied repo, which is the same as the lowest net basis. Net basis is roughly the price of the delivery options.

Step 4 · Position value

Like every future PV = (Fnow − Ftrade)/100 × $100,000 × n

Level 4 Desk reality

  • Conversion factor formula. CBOT computes CF as the price per $1 of the bond at a 6% yield, with maturity measured from the first day of the delivery month and rounded down to whole quarters. It is fixed for the life of the contract and published by the exchange. Our bond has 17 years and 9 months after rounding, giving 0.8510.
  • Which bond is CTD? When yields are below 6%, low-duration bonds tend to be CTD. Above 6%, high-duration bonds tend to be. A big yield move can switch the CTD, which gives the future negative convexity.
  • Delivery options. The seller picks the bond (quality option), the day in the delivery month (timing option) and can deliver after the futures market closes (wild card). These are all worth something to the short, which is why net basis > 0.
  • Contract family. CBOT 2Y, 5Y, 10Y, Ultra 10Y, Bond and Ultra Bond, plus Eurex Schatz, Bobl, Bund, Buxl and ICE Long Gilt. Each has its own basket rule and notional coupon (6% for US, 6% for Bund, 4% for Gilt).
  • Naming. US 20yr Bond Mar27 is the March 2027 contract on the long Treasury basket. The CTD's static data and repo come from the bond and repo pages of the system.

Worked example

On 23 Nov 2026 (settlement 24 Nov) you are long 25 US Bond Mar27 futures bought at 118-08. The market is 118-24. The CTD is a hypothetical 4.625% Treasury maturing 15 Feb 2045 at a clean price of 101.50, and repo is 3.70%. The last coupon was 15 Aug 2026 (101 days ago, in a 184-day period). The next coupon of 2.3125 is paid on 15 Feb 2027. Delivery is 31 Mar 2027, 127 days away and 44 days after that coupon, inside a 181-day period.

  1. Accrued interest and dirty price today.
    AI0 = 2.3125 × 101/184 = 1.2694 dirty = 101.50 + 1.2694 = 102.7694
  2. Forward dirty price at delivery.
    Bfwd = 102.7694 × (1 + 0.037×127/360) − 2.3125 × (1 + 0.037×44/360) = 101.7878
  3. Accrued at delivery and forward clean price.
    AIdel = 2.3125 × 44/181 = 0.5622 forward clean = 101.7878 − 0.5622 = 101.2257
  4. Fair futures price, ignoring delivery options.
    Ffair = 101.2257 / 0.8510 = 118.949 (≈ 118-30)
  5. Basis at the market price 118.75.
    gross = 101.50 − 118.75×0.8510 = 0.4438 carry = 0.2743 net = 0.1694 implied repo = 3.23%
    Implied repo (3.23%) is below actual repo (3.70%). The 0.17 net basis is what the market pays for the short's delivery options.
  6. Position value and DV01.
    PV = (118.75 − 118.25)/100 × 100,000 × 25 = $12,500 DV01 = 146.48 × 25 = $3,662

PV +$12,500. Position DV01 is $3,662 per bp, lost if yields rise.

Remember

  • The seller delivers the cheapest bond, so price the future off the CTD.
  • Fair price = (CTD forward dirty − accrued at delivery) ÷ conversion factor.
  • The CTD has the highest implied repo. Net basis ≈ delivery option value.
  • DV01 per contract = CTD DV01 ÷ CF. Hedge ratio = bond DV01 ÷ futures DV01.
