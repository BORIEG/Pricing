Non-deliverable swaps

An interest rate swap in a currency you are not allowed to pay or receive offshore. Every interest calculation happens in that currency, and every payment is made in US dollars.

Level 1 The idea

Some countries, such as Taiwan, Korea, India and Brazil, restrict who can hold their currency outside the country. A fund in London can have a view on Taiwanese interest rates and still not be allowed to move Taiwan dollars (TWD) around.

The non-deliverable swap (NDS) solves this. You and the bank agree an ordinary interest rate swap in TWD, as on the interest rate swaps page: one side pays a fixed TWD rate, the other pays a floating TWD rate. On each payment date the two TWD amounts are netted. The net is then converted into US dollars at that day's official exchange rate, and only those dollars change hands.

Imagine a bet with a friend abroad, scored in their country's game tokens. You can't send each other tokens, so at the end of each round you look up what the tokens are worth in dollars and settle in dollars. The score is kept in tokens. The money moves in dollars.

So an NDS carries two risks: TWD interest rates (the score) and the USD/TWD exchange rate (the conversion).

Level 2 The cashflows

Take the receiver of a 2-year TWD NDS on TWD 500 million. Every quarter you are owed TWD 500m × 1.80% × ¼ at the fixed rate, and you owe TWD 500m × (3M fixing) × ¼ at the floating rate. There is no exchange of notional, because both legs are in the same currency.

A couple of business days before each payment, two things are observed: the TWD 3-month rate for the next period, and the USD/TWD fixing for this payment. The net TWD amount is divided by the FX fixing to get US dollars.

USD payment on date ti, for the fixed receiver USDi = N × (K − Li) × τiXi
N
TWD notional (500,000,000)
K
fixed TWD rate (1.80%)
Li
the TWD 3-month fixing for period i
τi
accrual fraction, days ÷ 365 for TWD
Xi
USD/TWD fixing for that payment (TWD per 1 USD, around 31.5)

Level 3 The PV formula

Two things are unknown in each payment: the rate Li and the FX fixing Xi. We replace each by its market forward, then discount the dollars on the USD curve.

Step 1 · forward TWD rate for each period (TWD projection curve) Fi = 1τi ( PTWD(ti−1)PTWD(ti) − 1 )
Step 2 · forward FX rate, read from the NDF market Xfwd(t) = S × DUSD(t)DTWD,NDF(t)

Here DTWD,NDF is the TWD discount curve implied by NDF prices. It is usually different from onshore TWD rates, because the offshore market cannot arbitrage the two. Now discount each converted payment with the USD OIS curve:

Step 3 · PV in USD for the fixed receiver PVUSD = Σi N (K − Fi) τiXfwd(ti) DUSD(ti) = 1S Σi N (K − Fi) τi DTWD,NDF(ti)

The two forms give the same number. Converting each future TWD amount at its FX forward and discounting in USD is the same as discounting in TWD on the NDF-implied curve and converting once at today's spot S. Use whichever is easier.

The par rate is the K that makes the PV zero. It works like an ordinary swap, with the NDF-implied curve as the discount curve:

Kpar = Σ Fi τi DTWD,NDF(ti)Σ τi DTWD,NDF(ti)

Level 4 Desk reality

  • Reading the trade description. TWD P TDCC-3M/R3.12575 % 17/Sep/36 USD reads: TWD notional, you Pay the 3M floating index that systems label TDCC-3M, you Receive 3.12575% fixed, maturity 17 Sep 2036, settled in USD. Korean versions use KSDA-3M (the KRW 3M CD rate).
  • Conventions (TWD). Quarterly on both legs, ACT/365 fixed. The FX conversion uses the official Taipei fixing (TAIFX1), observed two business days before payment.
  • Three curves. The onshore TWD curve projects the floating rate. The NDF-implied TWD curve (or equivalently USD OIS plus NDF forward points) converts and discounts. USD OIS discounts the dollars. Many systems bundle the second and third into one "TWD discounted in USD collateral" curve.
  • Quanto effect. If TWD rates and USD/TWD move together, the average of "net amount ÷ FX" is not exactly "forward amount ÷ forward FX". The correction is tiny on short tenors. Desks either ignore it or add a small quanto adjustment based on the rate-FX correlation.
  • Accrued legs. On a live trade the current period's floating rate has already fixed, so that coupon is known in TWD. Only its FX conversion is still unknown.

Worked example

On 23 Nov 2026 you are the receiver of a 2-year TWD NDS: TWD 500m, fixed 1.80%, quarterly. For simplicity every period is exactly ¼ year. Market (flat, continuously compounded): TWD onshore zero 1.70%, NDF-implied TWD zero 1.20%, USD OIS zero 3.80%, USD/TWD spot 31.50.

  1. Forward TWD rate. With a flat curve every quarter has the same forward.
    F = (e^(0.017×0.25) − 1) / 0.25 = 1.70362%
  2. Net TWD flow each quarter, for the receiver.
    500,000,000 × (1.80% − 1.70362%) × 0.25 = TWD 120,478
  3. First quarter (t = 0.25): convert at the FX forward and discount in USD.
    X(0.25) = 31.50 × e^((0.012−0.038)×0.25) = 31.2959 USD = 120,478 / 31.2959 = 3,849.64 × D_USD = e^(−0.038×0.25) = 0.990545 → USD 3,813.24
  4. Last quarter (t = 2): the TWD forward is lower, so each TWD buys more dollars.
    X(2) = 29.9039 USD = 4,028.84 × D_USD = 0.926816 → USD 3,734.00
  5. Add all 8 quarters, or use the shortcut: discount at the NDF curve and convert at spot.
    Σ τ·D_TWD,NDF = 1.97323 PV = 500,000,000 × (1.80% − 1.70362%) × 1.97323 / 31.50 = USD 30,188
  6. Risk.
    DV01 ≈ −USD 3,145 per bp of TWD rates FX delta ≈ −USD 299 if USD/TWD +1%

The receiver's NDS is worth +USD 30,188. The fixed rate is 9.6bp above the 1.7036% par rate, and 9.6bp × USD 3,145 per bp ≈ USD 30,200.

Remember

  • An NDS is a normal swap scored in the restricted currency and settled in USD at an FX fixing.
  • PV = Σ net local flow ÷ FX forward × USD discount factor. That equals discounting on the NDF-implied curve and converting at spot.
  • Its main risk is local-currency DV01. FX risk applies only to the PV itself.
  • The cross-currency version, with notional exchanges, is on the cross-currency swaps page.
