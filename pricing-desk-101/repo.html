Part 1 · Cash products

Repo & reverse repo

A short-term loan of cash secured by a bond. It is how bond positions are financed and how cash earns a safe return overnight. The pricing is simple interest. The skill lies in the collateral.

Level 1 The idea

You own a bond and need cash for two months. You "sell" the bond to a bank for cash today and promise to buy it back on a fixed date for a slightly higher price. The difference is the interest. That is a repo (sale and repurchase agreement). The bank, on the other side, is doing a reverse repo: it lends cash and holds your bond as security.

If you fail to pay, the bank keeps and sells the bond. Because the loan is secured, the interest rate (the repo rate) is low, close to the overnight rate.

A pawn shop. You hand over a watch, get cash, and come back later to pay the cash plus a fee to reclaim it. The shop lends a bit less than the watch is worth, in case its value drops. In repo that safety margin is called the haircut.

Level 2 The cashflows

A reverse repo, where you lend cash, has two cash legs. On the start date you pay the cash and receive the bond. On the end date you receive the cash plus interest and hand the bond back. Both cash amounts are known from the start.

Level 3 The PV formula

Step 1 · how much cash is lent C = face100 × Pdirty × (1 − h)
Step 2 · the repurchase amount (simple interest) R = C × ( 1 + rrepo × d360 )
Pdirty
collateral bond's dirty price (clean + accrued), per 100 face
h
haircut, e.g. 2% for an investment-grade corporate, 0–1% for Treasuries, 10%+ for high yield or EM
rrepo
agreed repo rate. ACT/360 in USD and EUR, ACT/365 in GBP.
d
days from start to end
Step 3 · PV today for the cash lender before start: PV = −C × D(T1) + R × D(T2)
after start: PV = R × D(T2)     MTM = R × D(T2) − C

D(t) comes from today's market repo or OIS rate for the remaining term: D = 1 ÷ (1 + rmkt × days left ÷ 360). The MTM is the value over and above the cash you handed over. It has two parts: the interest earned so far, and a small gain or loss because market rates moved since you traded.

A repo, where you borrow cash, is the same with every sign flipped.

Level 4 Desk reality

  • GC vs special. General collateral (GC) is any bond from an accepted basket and pays close to OIS. A bond that many people want to borrow, for example to deliver into a short sale, goes special. Its repo rate drops, sometimes below zero, because lenders of that bond accept less interest on the cash.
  • Margin calls. If the collateral's value falls below cash × (1 + haircut), the borrower posts more bonds or cash. This happens daily under the GMRA (Global Master Repurchase Agreement), the standard legal contract.
  • Open vs term. A term repo has a fixed end date and rate. An open repo rolls every day at a rate that can be reset, and either side can end it with notice.
  • Who keeps the bond risk. The repo borrower still owns the economics of the bond: its price moves and its coupons are paid back to them. The cash lender is only exposed if the borrower defaults and the bond has fallen by more than the haircut.

"144A" means the bond was privately placed with large institutional buyers. CollateralPool GMRA groups the collateral held under one GMRA agreement. It has no PV of its own, because its value is the sum of the pieces it holds.

Worked example

On 20 Nov 2026 we lent cash for 63 days, to 22 Jan 2027, at 4.10%. The collateral was $10m face of a bond with a dirty price of 98.50 and a 2% haircut. Today is 23 Nov 2026, so 60 days are left, and 60-day repo now trades at 3.95%.

  1. Cash lent.
    C = 10,000,000/100 × 98.50 × (1 − 0.02) = $9,653,000
  2. Interest and repurchase amount.
    interest = 9,653,000 × 0.041 × 63/360 = $69,260.28 R = $9,722,260.28
  3. Discount factor for the 60 days left, at today's market rate.
    D = 1 / (1 + 0.0395 × 60/360) = 0.993460
  4. PV and MTM.
    PV = 9,722,260.28 × 0.993460 = $9,658,674 MTM = 9,658,674 − 9,653,000 = +$5,674
  5. Explain the MTM. Three days of interest is 9,653,000 × 0.041 × 3/360 = $3,298. The rest, about $2,376, comes from locking 4.10% when the market now pays 3.95%.
  6. DV01: reprice with the market rate at 3.96%.
    PV falls by $160

The reverse repo is worth $9,658,674, which is +$5,674 above the cash lent.

Remember

  • Repo = borrow cash against a bond. Reverse repo = lend cash and take the bond.
  • Cash = collateral value × (1 − haircut). Repurchase = cash × (1 + rate × days/360).
  • PV after start = repurchase amount × discount factor. MTM = accrued interest + rate move.
  • The bond's price risk stays with the borrower. The lender only cares about collateral cover.
