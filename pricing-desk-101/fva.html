Part 7 · Exotics & volatility

Forward volatility agreements

A deal to buy an at-the-money straddle at a future date, at a volatility fixed today. It is the volatility version of an FRA: you lock in tomorrow's vol the way an FRA locks in tomorrow's interest rate.

Level 1 The idea

A straddle is a call plus a put at the same strike. It makes money if the exchange rate moves a lot in either direction, so its price is mostly a bet on volatility, the size of future moves.

Suppose you think EUR/JPY will be jumpy in spring 2027, but you don't want to pay for options today and suffer time decay over the winter. With an FVA you agree today: "On 17 March 2027, I will buy a 3-month at-the-money straddle priced at 9.10% vol, whatever the market vol is that day." If market vol on 17 March is 11%, you get an option worth 11% for the price of 9.10%. If it is 8%, you overpay.

It is like agreeing in November on the price of a ski pass for the March holidays. If heavy snow makes passes expensive in March, your locked price is a bargain. Nobody knows yet which day will be snowy. You only care about the overall mood of the season.

The strike of the straddle is not fixed today. It is set on 17 March at the forward rate of that day ("at the money"). That keeps the trade a clean bet on vol and removes most of the bet on direction.

Level 2 The cashflows

Today: nothing is exchanged. On the strike date T1 the straddle is struck at the then-forward K = F(T1, T2), and the buyer pays its premium computed at the agreed vol (9.10%). On the expiry T2 the straddle pays |ST2 − K| per EUR of notional.

In practice FVAs are often cash-settled at T1: the two sides exchange the difference between the straddle priced at market vol and priced at 9.10%.

Level 3 The PV formula

Step 1 · Forward volatility from today's vol curve

Today the market quotes an at-the-money vol σ1 for options to T1 and σ2 for options to T2. Variance (vol squared times time) adds up over time like distance on a trip. So the variance expected between T1 and T2 is the total minus the first part:

Forward variance and forward vol σf² = σ2² T2 − σ1² T1T2 − T1

This is the same trick as the forward rate in FRAs, applied to variance. It needs σ2²T2 > σ1²T1, otherwise the vol curve would allow an arbitrage.

Step 2 · Price of an at-the-money-forward straddle

With K = F, the Black formula from the toolkit simplifies. Both d1 and d2 become ±½σ√τ, and the call and the put are worth the same:

ATMF straddle per unit of foreign notional, option life τ = T₂ − T₁ Straddle(σ) = 2 F D [ 2N(½σ√τ) − 1 ]  ≈  0.8 × σ √τ × F × D
0.8
comes from 2 × 0.3989 (the bell-curve height at zero); the approximation is excellent for short options

Step 3 · FVA value

PV for the buyer (in JPY) PV = N × [ Straddle(σf) − Straddle(Kvol) ]  ≈  N × 0.8 × (σf − Kvol) × √τ × F × D

Using today's forward F to T2 is legitimate. The straddle's value on the strike date is proportional to the spot level then, and the average of that, discounted, is exactly today's F × D.

An FVA is long the long-dated vol and short the short-dated vol. Raise σ2 and the forward vol rises. Raise σ1 and it falls. That makes it the natural tool for trading the shape of the vol term structure.

Level 4 Desk reality

  • Reading the trade description. FVA Straddle European 9.100000 % EUR:JPY|17/Mar/27 is an FVA on a European EUR/JPY straddle at 9.10% vol with strike date 17 Mar 2027. The option tenor after the strike date (often 1M or 3M) is in the trade details.
  • ATM convention. FX desks quote the at-the-money strike as delta-neutral straddle (DNS) for most pairs, slightly away from the forward. The difference from ATMF is small for EUR/JPY at 3 months.
  • Forward smile. Step 1 only uses at-the-money vols. Under stochastic-vol models, the vol that prevails on 17 March is itself uncertain. A straddle is close to linear in vol, so this "vol of vol" effect is small but not zero. Desks mark FVAs with a stochastic or local-stochastic vol model and quote the difference as a convexity adjustment.
  • Event vol. If a known event (a BoJ meeting, an election) falls between T1 and T2, the forward vol is higher than smooth interpolation suggests. Traders use FVAs to buy that event vol without paying for the quiet months before it.
  • After the strike date the FVA becomes an ordinary straddle and is risk-managed like any FX vanilla.

Worked example

On 23 Nov 2026 we buy an FVA on EUR 10m. The strike date is 17 Mar 2027 (114 days) and the straddle expires 3 months later on 17 Jun 2027 (206 days). The strike vol is 9.10%. Illustrative market: ATM vol 9.50% to March, 9.80% to June, EUR/JPY forward to June 168.00, JPY rate 0.80%.

  1. Times.
    T1 = 114/365 = 0.312329 T2 = 206/365 = 0.564384 τ = T2 − T1 = 0.252055
  2. Forward vol.
    σf² = (0.098² × 0.564384 − 0.095² × 0.312329) / 0.252055 = 0.0103215 σf = 10.1595%
    Vol from March to June is higher than either quoted vol, because the June vol has to "catch up" over a short window.
  3. Discount factor.
    D = e^(−0.008 × 0.564384) = 0.995495
  4. Straddle prices per EUR, exact formula.
    Straddle(10.1595%) = 2 × 168 × 0.995495 × [2N(½ × 0.101595 × √0.252055) − 1] = 6.805490 JPY Straddle(9.10%) = 2 × 168 × 0.995495 × [2N(½ × 0.091 × √0.252055) − 1] = 6.095924 JPY
  5. PV.
    PV = 10,000,000 × (6.805490 − 6.095924) = JPY 7,095,651
  6. Check with the 0.8 rule.
    10,000,000 × 0.8 × (0.101595 − 0.091) × √0.252055 × 168 × 0.995495 = JPY 7,116,545

The FVA is worth +JPY 7.10m (about EUR 42k) to the buyer. The market's forward vol of 10.16% is above the locked 9.10%. Each extra point of forward vol adds about JPY 6.7m.

Try it

Flatten the vol curve (set both vols equal) and the forward vol equals them. Make the short vol higher than the long one and watch the forward vol drop below both.

Remember

  • An FVA locks today the vol of an at-the-money straddle that starts later.
  • Forward variance = (σ₂²T₂ − σ₁²T₁) ÷ (T₂ − T₁), just like a forward rate.
  • PV ≈ N × 0.8 × (σ_f − K_vol) × √τ × F × D.
  • It is long the long-dated vol and short the short-dated vol, with almost no delta or gamma before the strike date.
  • Forward smile and vol of vol make the true price differ slightly from this formula.
