Part 5 · Mortgages

MBS, TBAs & CMOs

Bonds made of thousands of home loans. Each month the homeowners' payments flow through to the investor. Because homeowners can repay early whenever they like, you never know exactly when your money comes back. Pricing an MBS is mostly about guessing that.

Level 1 The idea

When an American family buys a house, they usually take a 30-year fixed-rate mortgage. Every month they pay the same amount, part interest and part repayment of the loan. Agencies backed by the US government (Ginnie Mae, Fannie Mae, Freddie Mac) buy thousands of these loans, put them in a pool, and sell shares of the pool to investors. That share is a mortgage-backed security, or pass-through, because the payments pass straight through to you. The agency guarantees you against defaults, so the only risk left is timing.

Timing is uncertain because families can repay their whole loan at any time with no penalty. They do this when they move house, or when rates fall and they refinance into a cheaper loan. This is called prepayment.

Imagine lending your bike to 1,000 classmates who each pay you a little every week. Anyone can hand the bike back early and stop paying. If bikes suddenly get cheaper elsewhere, lots of them return yours at once, just when you would have liked to keep collecting. That is the mortgage investor's problem.

The desk trades three flavours, all priced from the same monthly cash-flow engine:

  • Pass-through pools (type Mbs, Tba once allocated). A specific pool, identified by a pool number.
  • TBA ("to be announced", type Tba). A forward contract to buy or sell some pool with a given agency, coupon and maturity, for settlement in a future month. The exact pools are only announced two days before settlement.
  • CMOs (type Mbs, structured). The pool's cash flows are cut into tranches that get paid in a set order.

Level 2 The cashflows

Each month k the investor receives three things:

  • Interest at the pool's net coupon c on the remaining balance. The homeowners pay a higher rate, the WAC (weighted-average coupon). The difference pays the servicer and the agency guarantee.
  • Scheduled principal, the repayment part of the fixed monthly mortgage payment.
  • Prepayments, the loans repaid early that month.

Prepayment speed is quoted as a CPR (conditional prepayment rate): the fraction of the remaining balance repaid early over a year. Cash flows are monthly, so we convert it to a monthly rate, the SMM (single monthly mortality):

Annual to monthly prepayment rateSMM = 1 − (1 − CPR)1/12

The PSA convention is an older way to quote speed: 100% PSA ramps CPR from 0.2% in month 1 up to 6% at month 30, then stays at 6%. "200 PSA" means double that curve.

Level 3 The PV formula

Step 1 · One month of cash flow

Start the month with balance Bk−1 and n months left. Let g = WAC ÷ 12.

Monthly cash flow to the investor mortgage payment Mk = Bk−1 × g1 − (1 + g)−n
scheduled principal SPk = Mk − Bk−1 × g
interest to investor Ik = Bk−1 × c ÷ 12
prepayment PPk = SMM × (Bk−1 − SPk)
cash flow CFk = Ik + SPk + PPk new balance Bk = Bk−1 − SPk − PPk

Step 2 · Discount every month

Price at a yield y (monthly compounding) PV = Σk = 1 … 360 CFk(1 + y/12)k price = 100 × PV ÷ current face

Step 3 · Effective duration and negative convexity

When rates fall, homeowners refinance and CPR jumps. Your cash comes back early, just when you would rather keep earning the old coupon. So the price rises less than an ordinary bond's. That is negative convexity. To measure it, move the yield up and down by Δ and change the CPR with it, then reprice:

Effective duration and convexity Dureff = PV(y − Δ) − PV(y + Δ)2 × PV × Δ Conveff = PV(y − Δ) + PV(y + Δ) − 2 PVPV × Δ2

The weighted-average life (WAL) is the average time until each dollar of principal comes back: WAL = Σ (k/12) × principalk ÷ Σ principalk.

TBA: a forward on a pool

A TBA trade fixes a price Ptrade today for delivery on the settlement date of a future month, which follows the SIFMA calendar (mid-month for 30-year UMBS). It is a forward contract, so:

TBA PV for the buyer PV = Face × PTBA, market − Ptrade100 × D(settle)

The seller chooses which pools to deliver, so they deliver the least valuable ones, the cheapest to deliver. The TBA price is therefore the price of the worst pools that qualify. Pools with slow, predictable prepayments (small loan sizes, certain states, low-income borrowers) trade at a premium to TBA, called the pay-up. Those are specified pools.

The price difference between this month's and next month's settlement is the drop. Selling the front month and buying the next one is a dollar roll, the main way to finance MBS. A fair drop is roughly one month of carry:

Fair drop (points per month, approximate) drop ≈ c × 10012 − P × rrepo12 + SMM × (100 − P)

CMOs: slicing the pool

A CMO (collateralised mortgage obligation) runs the same pool cash flows through a waterfall. In a sequential deal, tranche A gets all principal until it is repaid, then B, then C, so A is short and C is long. An IO (interest only) gets just the interest, and its value falls when prepayments speed up. A PO (principal only) gets just principal, and its value rises with faster prepayments. To price any CMO class, project the pool cash flows (Step 1), apply the deal's payment rules each month to find that class's cash flows, then discount (Step 2).

Level 4 Desk reality

  • Prepayment models, not one CPR. Desks project CPR month by month from a model driven by the refinancing incentive (WAC minus current mortgage rate), loan age, seasonality, burnout and housing turnover. The yield curve is simulated with hundreds of rate paths.
  • OAS. The standard price measure is the option-adjusted spread: the constant spread over the simulated Treasury/SOFR paths that makes the average discounted cash flow equal the market price. It is the "yield" that already accounts for prepayment.
  • Payment delay. Investors are paid later than homeowners pay: about 45 days for Ginnie I, 50 for Ginnie II, and 55 days for UMBS (Fannie/Freddie). This shifts every cash flow and lowers the price slightly.
  • Factor. Pools are quoted on original face. Current face = original face × pool factor, which is published monthly. Accrued interest is 30/360 from the 1st of the month.
  • Prices in 32nds. 100-16 means 100 + 16/32 = 100.50. A "+" adds 1/64.
  • Reading trade descriptions. STIP G2SF 7.5 8/26 G2 DU6841 reads as a Ginnie Mae II single-family (G2SF) 7.5% pool, pool number DU6841, dated 8/26. STIP most likely marks a stipulated (specified-pool) trade rather than a generic TBA. STRU MS-3149 SG is a structured CMO class: deal 3149, class SG. Class codes like S often denote inverse-floating or interest-only pieces, so check the deal prospectus before assuming anything.

Worked example

We price USD 1,000,000 current face of a pass-through: WAC 6.00%, net coupon 5.50%, 360 months remaining, constant 10% CPR, discounted at a 5.40% yield (monthly compounding, no payment delay). For risk we assume CPR rises 5% for every 100bp fall in rates.

  1. Monthly prepayment rate.
    SMM = 1 − (1 − 0.10)^(1/12) = 0.874161%
  2. Month 1, with g = 0.06/12 = 0.005.
    payment = 1,000,000 × 0.005 / (1 − 1.005^−360) = 5,995.51 sched. principal = 5,995.51 − 1,000,000 × 0.005 = 995.51 interest = 1,000,000 × 0.055 / 12 = 4,583.33 prepay = 0.00874161 × (1,000,000 − 995.51) = 8,732.91 cash flow = 4,583.33 + 995.51 + 8,732.91 = 14,311.75
  3. Roll the balance forward (1,000,000 − 995.51 − 8,732.91 = 990,271.58) and repeat for 360 months. Discount each month at (1 + 0.054/12)−k and add up.
    PV = 1,005,581.75 → price = 100.558 WAL = 7.68 years
  4. Effective duration. Bump the yield ±50bp and move CPR the other way (−50bp → 12.5% CPR, +50bp → 7.5% CPR).
    PV(4.90%, 12.5% CPR) = 1,029,901.27 PV(5.90%, 7.5% CPR) = 974,797.48 Dur_eff = (1,029,901.27 − 974,797.48) / (2 × 1,005,581.75 × 0.005) = 5.48 Conv_eff = (1,029,901.27 + 974,797.48 − 2 × 1,005,581.75) / (1,005,581.75 × 0.005²) = −257
  5. Compare with a fixed 10% CPR, as if the bond were an ordinary bond. Then the duration is 5.58 and the convexity is +58. The prepayment option flips convexity negative.
  6. TBA mark. We bought USD 10m UMBS 30Y 5.5 for December settlement at 100-16. It now trades at 100-24, and settlement is 21 days away (4% OIS).
    PV = 10,000,000 × (100.75 − 100.50)/100 × e^(−0.04×21/365) = 25,000 × 0.997701 = USD 24,943
  7. Fair dollar-roll drop at a 4% repo rate, price 100.75, using the prepayment part of SMM.
    drop ≈ 0.4583 − 0.3358 + 0.00874 × (100 − 100.75) = 0.1159 points ≈ 3.7/32nds

The pool is worth 100.558 (USD 1,005,582 per USD 1m). It has an effective duration of 5.48, a DV01 of USD 551 per bp, and negative convexity.

Remember

  • Monthly cash flow = interest at the net coupon + scheduled principal + prepayment, where SMM = 1 − (1 − CPR)1/12.
  • Price = discounted sum of the projected cash flows. On the desk the projection comes from a prepayment model across many rate paths (OAS).
  • Prepayments speed up when rates fall, which creates negative convexity. Measure risk with effective duration.
  • A TBA is a forward on the cheapest deliverable pool. A CMO is the same cash flows split by a waterfall.
