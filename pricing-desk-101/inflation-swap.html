Part 3 · Swaps

Zero-coupon inflation swaps

One payment, years from now: realised inflation against a fixed rate agreed today. The fixed rate that makes the swap fair is the market's breakeven inflation, the most-watched inflation number on a rates desk.

Level 1 The idea

A pension fund must pay pensions that rise with prices. If inflation runs hot for 15 years, its bills explode. It wants protection.

In a zero-coupon inflation swap, the fund and a bank agree a fixed rate today, say 3.335% a year. Nothing happens for 15 years. At the end, the bank pays the fund the actual rise in the price index over those years, and the fund pays the bank what 3.335% a year compounded would have given. If real inflation beat 3.335%, the fund wins, which offsets its higher pension bill.

You and a friend bet on how much a cinema ticket will cost in 15 years. You lock in "it grows 3.335% a year". Your friend takes whatever the real price turns out to be. In 15 years whoever is wrong pays the difference, scaled up to a notional of GBP 10m.

Level 2 The cashflows

There is exactly one exchange, at maturity, so it is called zero-coupon. Both amounts are percentages of the notional N. The notional itself is never exchanged.

The two amounts paid at maturity T fixed leg = N × [ (1 + K)T − 1 ]     inflation leg = N × [ I(T)Ibase − 1 ]
K
fixed rate (3.335%), compounded annually over the whole number of years T (15)
Ibase
the index level for the start date, after the lag (see Level 4)
I(T)
the index level for the maturity date, with the same lag

Level 3 The PV formula

The only unknown is the final index I(T). As usual, replace it with its forward, which comes from the market's inflation swap rates for each maturity.

Step 1 · forward index from the market breakeven b for the remaining time Ifwd(T) = Iref × (1 + b)Trem
Iref
base index of a swap starting today. It is the latest index used under the lag.
b
market zero-coupon inflation swap rate for Trem years (the breakeven)
Trem
years from today to maturity (12.82)
Step 2 · PV for the fixed receiver (inflation payer), discounted on SONIA PV = N × D(Trem) × [ (1 + K)T − Ifwd(T)Ibase ]

The two "− 1" terms cancel. For a brand-new trade, Ibase = Iref and T = Trem, so:

PVnew = N × D(T) × [ (1 + K)T − (1 + b)T ]    ⇒    fair K = b

The fair fixed rate of a new ZC inflation swap is the breakeven inflation rate. It is called that because a nominal bond and an inflation-linked bond of the same maturity give the same return if realised inflation comes out at exactly this rate.

Level 4 Desk reality

  • Index lag. Price indices are published weeks after the month they measure, so swaps reference an older month. UK RPI swaps use a 2-month lag: a 15 Sep start uses July's RPI. Systems write this as Mar-2M on LPI trades. US CPI uses a 3-month lag, interpolated daily between two months. Euro HICPx (shown as EUR … CPI) and French CPI (FRCPI) use a 3-month lag, monthly, without interpolation.
  • Reading the trade description. Receive|3.335 %|Pay|GBP|RPI|15/Sep/24|15/Sep/39 means you receive the fixed 3.335% and pay RPI, start 15 Sep 2024, maturity 15 Sep 2039. Many trades are already started, so their base index is already fixed and only the final index is unknown.
  • Seasonality. Prices rise more in some months, for example January sales reversals and April utility bills in the UK. The forward index curve built from annual swap maturities needs a monthly seasonal pattern added. Without it, fixings in other months are mis-priced.
  • Curve building. The inflation curve is bootstrapped from ZC swap quotes at 1, 2, 3 … 50 years. Near-term fixings come from economists' forecasts (the desk's "fixings curve").
  • Discounting. SONIA for GBP, SOFR for USD, €STR for EUR, all collateralised. Payment is a few business days after the maturity date.
  • Convexity. A ZC swap pays the index ratio exactly at its natural date, so no convexity adjustment is needed. Year-on-year swaps, as on the LPI page, do need one.

Worked example

We price an example trade on 23 Nov 2026: receive 3.335% fixed, pay RPI, GBP 10m, 15 Sep 2024 to 15 Sep 2039 (T = 15). Index values and market rates are illustrative: base RPI (Jul-24) 387.5, base RPI for a swap starting today 408.0, market 13-year RPI breakeven 3.30%, SONIA zero 3.90% continuously compounded.

  1. Time left.
    T_rem = days(23 Nov 2026 → 15 Sep 2039) / 365 = 4,679 / 365 = 12.8192 years
  2. Forward final index.
    I_fwd = 408.0 × 1.033^12.8192 = 408.0 × 1.516193 = 618.607
  3. The two amounts expected at maturity.
    fixed = 10,000,000 × (1.03335^15 − 1) = 10,000,000 × 0.635730 = GBP 6,357,301 inflation = 10,000,000 × (618.607/387.5 − 1) = 10,000,000 × 0.596405 = GBP 5,964,047
  4. Net to you (fixed receiver), then discount.
    net = 6,357,301 − 5,964,047 = GBP 393,255 D = e^(−0.039×12.8192) = 0.606562 PV = 393,255 × 0.606562 = GBP 238,533
  5. Risks.
    IE01 ≈ −GBP 12,023 per bp of breakeven DV01 ≈ −GBP 306 per bp of SONIA
  6. Breakeven for this trade: the average inflation from now that would make the net zero.
    π* = (1.635730 × 387.5 / 408.0)^(1/12.8192) − 1 = 3.496%

The swap is worth +GBP 238,533 to the fixed receiver. The market expects 3.30% inflation a year from here, while this trade only loses money if inflation averages more than 3.50%.

Remember

  • One payment at maturity: N[(1+K)T − 1] against N[I(T)/Ibase − 1].
  • PV = N × D × [(1+K)T − Ifwd/Ibase], with Ifwd = Iref(1+b)Trem.
  • The fair rate of a new swap is the breakeven inflation rate.
  • Watch the index lag and seasonality. The main risk is IE01.
