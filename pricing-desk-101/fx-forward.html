Part 1 · Cash products

FX spot, forwards & NDFs

Swapping one currency for another, now or on an agreed future date. The forward price is fixed by interest rates alone, which makes this the cleanest example of "no free lunch" pricing in the course.

Level 1 The idea

An exchange rate is the price of one currency in another. "USD/ZAR = 18.20" means one US dollar costs 18.20 South African rand. The first currency (USD) is the base. The second (ZAR) is the quote currency, the one the price is expressed in.

  • Spot: exchange the two currencies now. "Now" means two business days later (T+2) because payments take time to arrange.
  • Forward: agree the rate today and exchange on a later date. Nothing changes hands until then.
  • NDF (non-deliverable forward): same as a forward, but for currencies that cannot move freely offshore (TWD, KRW, INR, BRL). No rand or won is delivered. Only the profit or loss is paid, in dollars.

You book holiday money in November for a February trip and lock today's price with the bureau. Whatever happens to the rand, you collect your dollars in February at the agreed rate. The bureau won't simply give you today's rate, though. It adjusts it for the interest each currency earns in the meantime.

Level 2 The cashflows

A forward has two cashflows on the same day, in two currencies. You receive one notional and pay the other. Both amounts are known today, since the rate is locked.

NDF settlement, paid in USD on the settlement date N × Xfix − KXfix
N

K

Xfix

Level 3 The PV formula

Why the forward isn't just spot. You could get dollars in February two ways. One is to lock a forward today. The other is to borrow rand now, buy dollars spot, and leave the dollars on deposit until February. Both must cost the same, or traders would borrow one way and lend the other for free money. Rand earns more interest than dollars, so the forward rate must be above spot to make up for it. This is covered interest parity.

Step 1 · forward rate from spot and two discount factors F = S × Dbase(T)Dquote(T)  =  S × 1 + rquote × d/Bquote1 + rbase × d/Bbase
S
spot rate (quote per base) for the spot date
d
days from the spot date to delivery
B
day-count base: 360 for USD and EUR, 365 for ZAR, GBP, JPY and most EM currencies
D(T)
discount factors from spot to delivery in each currency (see the toolkit)

Traders quote the gap as forward points: (F − S) × 10,000 for most pairs (× 100 for JPY pairs).

Step 2 · PV of a forward where you buy N of the base currency at K PVquote = N × (F − K) × Dquote(today → T)     PVbase = PVquote ÷ S

The same thing written as two legs: PV = N × Dbase × S − N × K × Dquote. The first term is the dollars you receive converted at spot, and the second is the rand you pay.

Step 3 · PV of an NDF, in USD PVUSD = N × F − KF × DUSD(today → T)

An FX swap is a spot (or near-date) trade plus the opposite forward. Price each leg with Step 2 and add them. Its value comes almost entirely from the interest-rate gap, not from the spot rate.

Level 4 Desk reality

  • Spot dates. Most pairs are T+2. USD/CAD, USD/TRY, USD/PHP and USD/RUB are T+1. The date must be a business day in both currencies, and USD holidays also matter for crosses settled through dollars.
  • Which way round. EUR, GBP, AUD and NZD are quoted as base against USD (EURUSD = 1.08). Almost everything else is USD-based (USDJPY, USDZAR). Always check whether to multiply or divide.
  • Discounting. Collateral is usually USD. The foreign "rate" in the parity formula is then the foreign OIS rate plus the cross-currency basis, and in practice desks simply read forward points from the market. See cross-currency swaps.
  • NDF dates. Fixing date = settlement minus two business days, using the onshore calendar. After fixing, the NDF is just a known USD cashflow.
  • Trade descriptions. FX USD:ZAR|9/Feb/27 is a deliverable forward (or spot) with its value date. NDF USD:TWD|9/Dec/26 is an NDF with its settlement date. Each forward is booked as its own product, with its own value date.

Worked example

Today is Monday 23 Nov 2026, so spot settles on 25 Nov. USD/ZAR spot is 18.2000, USD rates are 4.30% (ACT/360) and ZAR rates are 7.00% (ACT/365). We bought USD 10m for 9 Feb 2027 at 18.3500.

  1. Days. Spot to delivery is 76 days. Today to delivery is 78 days.
  2. Forward rate.
    F = 18.20 × (1 + 0.07×76/365) / (1 + 0.043×76/360) = 18.2992 (points +991.6)
  3. Rand discount factor from today.
    D_ZAR = 1 / (1 + 0.07×78/365) = 0.985262
  4. PV in rand, then dollars.
    PV = 10,000,000 × (18.2992 − 18.3500) × 0.985262 = −ZAR 500,951 = −USD 27,525
  5. NDF: bought USD 5m against TWD at 31.20 for 9 Dec 2026. Spot 31.00, TWD 1.60% (ACT/365), USD 4.30%, 14 days from spot, 16 from today.
    F = 31.00 × (1 + 0.016×14/365)/(1 + 0.043×14/360) = 30.9672
    PV = 5,000,000 × (30.9672 − 31.20)/30.9672 × 0.998093 = −USD 37,510

The USD/ZAR forward is worth −ZAR 500,951 (−USD 27,525): we locked 18.35 when the fair forward is 18.30. The TWD NDF is worth −USD 37,510.

Try it

Remember

  • Forward = spot × (1 + quote rate × time) ÷ (1 + base rate × time). The higher-yielding currency trades at a forward discount.
  • PV = notional × (fair forward − locked rate) × discount factor.
  • An NDF pays only the difference, in USD, divided by the fixing.
  • Risk is mostly FX delta, plus a small DV01 in each currency.
