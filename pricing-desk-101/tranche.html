Part 4 · Credit

Index tranches

Take the losses of a CDS index and cut them into layers. The bottom layer is hit first, the top layer almost never. Each layer is a separate trade, and its price depends on one new idea: how much the companies tend to default together.

Level 1 The idea

Picture the 125 companies of iTraxx Europe Main as a stack of 100 floors of losses. Every default adds a little water to the building, starting at the ground floor. A tranche is the insurance on a range of floors.

  • The equity tranche (0–3%) takes the first 3% of pool losses. It floods with just a few defaults.
  • The mezzanine tranches (3–6%, 6–12%) start losing only once the floors below are full.
  • The senior tranche (12–100%) needs a catastrophe: well over 20% of the companies defaulting at 40% recovery.

The example trade Tr:6-12 sells protection on the 6–12% layer. It loses money only when pool losses pass 6%, and it is wiped out at 12%.

It is like a group of friends splitting the cost of a broken window. The first friend pays the first EUR 30, the second pays from EUR 30 to EUR 60, and so on. The first friend pays for almost every accident. The last friend only pays if the whole house falls down.

Level 3 The PV formula

Step 1 · Expected tranche loss drives everything

If we know the expected tranche loss ETL(t) = average of TL over all scenarios at each date, the legs look just like a CDS. ETL plays the role of the default probability.

Tranche legs (per unit of tranche notional) RPV01 = Σi τi D(ti) [ 1 − ETL(ti−1) + ETL(ti)2 ]
Prot = Σi D(ti) [ ETL(ti) − ETL(ti−1) ]
PVseller = N × ( c × RPV01 − Prot ) par spread = Prot ÷ RPV01

Step 2 · Why correlation matters

The index tells us the average pool loss, but not how spread out it is. If companies default independently, losses cluster tightly around the average: about 3% over 5 years here. The equity tranche then takes a big hit, and the senior tranche is safe. If companies default together (high correlation), most years see almost no loss but a few see huge losses. The senior tranche becomes risky, and equity actually becomes safer.

Step 3 · One-factor model (large homogeneous pool)

The market's standard model uses a single number M for "the state of the economy", drawn from a standard normal distribution. Given M, each company defaults independently with a probability that rises when M is low. With many identical names, the pool loss is then known exactly for each M:

Pool loss given the economy M (Vasicek) L(M) = (1 − R) × N( N−1(p) − √ρ M√(1 − ρ) ), p = 1 − e−λt, λ = Sindex1 − R
Expected tranche loss: average over the economy ETL(t) = E[min(L, D)] − E[min(L, A)]D − A, E[min(L, K)] = average of min(L(M), K) over M ~ normal
N( ), N−1( )
the normal distribution table and its inverse (see the toolkit)
ρ

E[min(L, K)] is the expected loss of a base tranche 0–K. Every tranche is the difference of two base tranches. The average over M is a simple numerical sum: the calculator uses 200 slices of the normal curve.

Level 4 Desk reality

  • Base correlation. No single ρ reprices all tranches. The market therefore quotes a separate correlation for each base tranche 0–3%, 0–6%, 0–12% and so on. That set is the base correlation curve, and it usually rises with the detachment point. A 6–12% tranche is priced as [0–12% at ρ12] minus [0–6% at ρ6]. Our calculator uses one flat correlation, called compound correlation, which is fine for learning.
  • Heterogeneous pool. Real models use each name's own spread curve and recovery, plus a recursion (or Fourier method) for the loss distribution of 125 different names. The large-pool formula is the closed-form approximation.
  • Quotes. Tranches trade with a fixed running coupon (often 100bp, or 500bp and 0bp for equity) plus an upfront in points. Super-senior and senior tranches can trade with upfront near zero.
  • Defaults and recovery eat the bottom of the structure. Recoveries also amortise the top tranche, because a recovered amount can never be lost again.
  • In the trade description, Tr:6-12 gives the attachment and detachment, and the series and coupon follow the index naming. The trade notional is the tranche notional (EUR 10m of a 6% wide tranche covers 10m ÷ 6% = EUR 167m of index).

Worked example

Today is 23 Nov 2026. We sell EUR 10m of protection on the 6–12% tranche of iTraxx Europe Main S46, with a 100bp running coupon and maturity 20 Dec 2031. Market inputs: index spread 60bp, R = 40%, flat correlation ρ = 30%, EUR OIS 2.00%.

  1. Hazard rate and default probability at maturity (t = 5.0767 years).
    λ = 0.0060 / 0.6 = 1.00% p = 1 − e^(−0.01 × 5.0767) = 4.950% expected pool loss = 0.6 × 4.950% = 2.97%
  2. Pool loss in three economies, using N−1(0.0495) = −1.6497.
    M = −2 (recession): L = 0.6 × N((−1.6497 + 0.5477×2)/0.8367) = 15.23% M = 0 (normal): L = 0.6 × N(−1.6497/0.8367) = 1.46% M = +2 (boom): L = 0.03%
    Only bad economies push losses past 6%.
  3. Average over all economies to get the base-tranche expected losses at maturity, then the tranche.
    E[min(L, 6%)] = 2.2576% E[min(L, 12%)] = 2.7497% ETL = (2.7497% − 2.2576%) / 6% = 8.203% of the tranche
  4. Repeat Step 3 for all 21 quarterly dates and sum the legs.
    RPV01 = 4.735328 Prot = 0.076825 par spread = 162.2bp
  5. Upfront and PV for the seller.
    upfront (buyer pays) = 0.076825 − 0.01 × 4.735328 = 2.947 points PV seller = 10,000,000 × (0.01 × 4.735328 − 0.076825) = −EUR 294,717
  6. Risks, by repricing.
    Corr01 (ρ 30% → 31%) = −EUR 15,904 CS01 (index 60 → 61bp) = −EUR 19,271

The 6–12% tranche is worth 162bp running. Selling it with a 100bp coupon means receiving 2.95 points upfront. The position's PV is −EUR 294,717, offset by that upfront.

Try it

Change the attachment, detachment and correlation. Try 0–3% and then 12–100%: the chart shows that rising correlation helps the equity seller and hurts the senior seller.

Remember

  • A tranche takes pool losses between A and D: TL = min(max(L − A, 0), D − A) ÷ (D − A).
  • Price it like a CDS with expected tranche loss in place of default probability.
  • Expected tranche loss needs the whole loss distribution, which needs a correlation. The one-factor Gaussian model is the market standard, quoted as base correlation.
  • Equity is long correlation, senior is short correlation. Back to CDS indices, or on to mortgages.
