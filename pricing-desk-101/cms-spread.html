Part 6 · Vanilla options

CMS spread options

An option on the steepness of the yield curve: the 30-year swap rate minus the 5-year swap rate. It needs two vols and a correlation, and the correlation is where the trading happens.

Level 1 The idea

Long-term rates are usually higher than short-term rates. The gap between them is the steepness of the curve. Traders call "30-year swap rate minus 5-year swap rate" the 30s5s spread. Today it might be 0.40%.

The steepness moves with the economy. Before recessions the curve often flattens or inverts (the spread falls below zero). In recoveries it steepens. A spread option pays on where that gap ends up:

  • A spread cap (call) pays if the spread ends above the strike: a bet on steepening.
  • A spread floor (put) pays if it ends below: a bet on flattening.
  • A straddle is both together. It pays whenever the spread moves far from the strike, in either direction. It is a bet on the size of the move, whichever way it goes.

Two siblings, aged 12 and 16, are 20cm apart in height today. You bet on the gap in a year. If they grow at the same speed, the gap barely changes. If one has a growth spurt and the other doesn't, the gap moves a lot. How much it can move depends on how independently they grow. That is correlation.

Level 2 The cashflows

A CMS rate (constant maturity swap rate) is the market swap rate for a fixed tenor, observed on one date. "CMS 30Y on 2 Aug 2027" is the 30-year swap rate published that day. It is a single number used to set a single payment.

The buyer pays a premium today. On the fixing date both CMS rates are observed, and the option may pay:

Payoff for one period, with spread X = CMS30Y − CMS5Y Cap: Nτ max(X − K, 0)    Floor: Nτ max(K − X, 0)    Straddle: Nτ |X − K|

Level 3 The PV formula

Step 1 · Forward CMS rates, with a convexity adjustment

The centre of each rate's bell curve is its forward swap rate from the swaptions page, plus a small convexity adjustment. A normal swap pays its rate every year for 30 years. A CMS payment pays the 30-year rate once. Moving the payment changes how it should be averaged, and the result is that the forward CMS rate sits slightly above the forward swap rate.

The adjustment grows with the rate's volatility, the time to fixing and the swap's tenor. Desks compute it by replicating the CMS payment with a strip of swaptions. For 30Y fixing in under a year it is a few basis points.

CMSfwd = forward swap rate + convexity adjustment

Step 2 · The forward spread

Xfwd = CMS30Y, fwd − CMS5Y, fwd

Step 3 · The spread's volatility

Each rate has its own normal vol σ30 and σ5 from the swaption market. When the two rates move together, their moves cancel in the spread. The correlation ρ (between −1 and +1) measures how much they move together:

Normal vol of a difference of two rates σX = √( σ302 + σ52 − 2ρσ30σ5 )

With ρ = 1 and equal vols the spread never moves. With ρ = 0 the vols add like the sides of a right-angled triangle. Real 30Y and 5Y rates have ρ around 0.8 to 0.9, so the spread is much calmer than either rate.

Step 4 · Bachelier on the spread

A spread can be negative, so the normal model is the natural choice. Treat X as one rate with vol σX:

d = Xfwd − KσX√T
Cap = NτD [ (Xfwd − K) N(d) + σX√T n(d) ]
Floor = NτD [ (K − Xfwd) N(−d) + σX√T n(d) ]
Straddle = Cap + Floor
T
time to the fixing date in years
D
OIS discount factor to the payment date
τ
accrual of the period the spread is paid on (1.0 for one annual period)

A long straddle gains when σX rises. σX rises when correlation falls. So the buyer of a spread straddle is short correlation. That exposure is why these trades exist: correlation cannot be traded any other simple way.

Level 4 Desk reality

  • Reading the trade description. StraddleSpread 1Y USD-SOFRCMP-30Y-5Y 0.2 % 2/Aug/27 means: straddle, 1-year period, USD SOFR swap CMS rates (30Y minus 5Y), strike 0.20%, ending 2 Aug 2027. We price one annual period whose spread is observed at the end, with τ = 1. Check the trade's schedule for the exact fixing and payment dates.
  • Multi-period trades. A 5-year spread cap is a strip of spread caplets, one per period, priced and summed exactly as on the caps & floors page.
  • Where correlation comes from. It is implied from broker quotes on spread options, and it differs by strike. Desks keep a correlation surface by expiry and strike, the same way they keep a vol surface.
  • Better models. Bachelier with one correlation is the market's quoting model. A more exact price uses a copula: it combines each rate's own smile with the correlation. The difference matters for far out-of-the-money strikes.
  • Convexity adjustments move. They depend on the swaption smile. When long-dated vols jump, the CMS forwards move too, so the 30Y leg carries extra vega.

Worked example

Valuation 23 Nov 2026. Long USD 100m 30s5s straddle, strike 0.20%, fixing 2 Aug 2027 (252 days, T = 0.69041), τ = 1. Illustrative market data: forward swap rates 30Y 4.15% and 5Y 3.78%, convexity adjustments +5bp and +2bp, normal vols 80bp and 95bp, correlation 0.85, SOFR OIS zero 3.5%.

  1. Forward CMS rates and spread.
    CMS30 = 4.15% + 0.05% = 4.20% CMS5 = 3.78% + 0.02% = 3.80% X = 0.40%
  2. Spread vol, in bp.
    σX = √(80² + 95² − 2 × 0.85 × 80 × 95) = √(6,400 + 9,025 − 12,920) = √2,505 = 50.05bp
  3. Bachelier inputs.
    σX√T = 0.005005 × √0.69041 = 0.0041587 d = (0.0040 − 0.0020) / 0.0041587 = 0.48092 N(d) = 0.684713 n(d) = 0.355376
  4. Cap and floor rate values.
    cap = 0.0020 × 0.684713 + 0.0041587 × 0.355376 = 0.0013694 + 0.0014779 = 0.0028473 floor = −0.0020 × 0.315287 + 0.0014779 = 0.0008473
  5. Straddle, discounted.
    straddle = 0.0028473 + 0.0008473 = 0.0036947 D = e^(−0.035 × 0.69041) = 0.976125 PV = 100,000,000 × 1 × 0.976125 × 0.0036947 = USD 360,644
  6. Correlation risk. Reprice with ρ = 0.86.
    PV(0.86) − PV(0.85) = −USD 8,858

The straddle is worth USD 360,644 (36bp of notional). USD 195,225 of it is intrinsic value: the spread is already 20bp above the strike (100m × 0.20% × 0.976125). The remaining USD 165,419 is time value, which depends heavily on correlation.

Remember

  • A CMS spread option pays on the difference between two swap rates observed on one date.
  • Forward CMS = forward swap rate + convexity adjustment (a few bp, larger for long tenors).
  • Spread vol: σX2 = σ12 + σ22 − 2ρσ1σ2. Then use Bachelier on the spread.
  • Long spread options are short correlation. That is the risk to watch.
