Part 3 · Swaps

Interest rate swaps & OIS

Two parties swap interest payments on the same notional for years: one pays a fixed rate, the other pays a floating rate. A swap is just a strip of FRAs, and once you can price one, you can price most of the rates world.

Level 1 The idea

A company has a $50m loan whose interest rate resets every year to the market rate. It is worried rates will rise. A bank offers a deal: "You pay us 3.40% a year, fixed, for five years. We pay you the floating rate each year." The company's floating payments to its lender are now covered by the bank. What is left is a fixed 3.40%.

That deal is an interest rate swap. Nobody exchanges the $50m. It only sizes the payments. The side paying fixed is the payer. The side receiving fixed is the receiver.

It is like swapping a pay-as-you-go phone plan for a fixed monthly contract. Your usage (the floating rate) still varies, but someone else now absorbs the variation, and you pay the same amount every month.

When the floating rate is an overnight rate compounded over each period (SOFR, SONIA, €STR), the swap is called an OIS (overnight index swap). Today almost all new USD, GBP and CHF swaps are OIS.

Level 2 The cashflows

Each year, on each payment date, two amounts are due:

  • Fixed leg: N × K × τi. Known today.
  • Floating leg: N × Ri × τi, where Ri is the compounded overnight rate for that period. Only known at the end of the period.

The two amounts on the same date are netted. Here is the 5-year payer swap from the worked example below.

Level 3 The PV formula

Step 1 · Value the fixed leg

Each fixed payment is known, so discount it. The sum of accrual × discount factor appears so often it has its own name, the annuity (or PV01 of the leg):

Fixed leg PVfixed = N × K × A,    A = Σi = 1..n τi D(Ti)

Step 2 · Value the floating leg

Replace each unknown Ri by its forward Fi = (D(Ti−1)/D(Ti) − 1)/τi, exactly as for an FRA. When the same OIS curve projects and discounts, something lovely happens: each term τiFiD(Ti) equals D(Ti−1) − D(Ti). The sum telescopes:

Floating leg (OIS, single curve) PVfloat = N Σi τi Fi D(Ti) = N × ( D(T0) − D(Tn) )

Step 3 · Par rate and PV

The par swap rate S is the fixed rate that makes both legs equal. It is what the market quotes on screens.

Par rate and PV of a payer swap (pay K, receive floating) S = D(T0) − D(Tn)A      PVpayer = N × (S − K) × A

A receiver swap has the opposite sign. This one line is the most used formula on a rates desk.

Step 4 · Multi-curve: IBOR or term-rate legs

For legs on EURIBOR, STIBOR, TAIFX or other term rates with bank credit risk, forwards come from a separate projection curve, while discounting stays on OIS. The telescoping trick no longer works:

General par rate S = Σj τjflt Fjproj DOIS(Tj)Σi τifix DOIS(Ti)

Fixed and floating legs may also have different frequencies (EUR: annual fixed vs 6M EURIBOR). That is why the sums use separate indices i and j. A spread on the floating leg simply adds N × spread × Aflt to that leg.

Level 4 Desk reality

  • Conventions. USD SOFR OIS: annual both legs, ACT/360, payment 2 business days after each period end, modified following, US government securities calendar. GBP SONIA: ACT/365F. EUR: fixed annual 30/360 against €STR, or 6M EURIBOR.
  • Spot start. A new swap usually starts two business days after trade (T+2), so D(T0) is slightly below 1.
  • Lookback and observation shift. To know the amount before paying it, some contracts use fixings from a few days earlier (a 2- or 5-day lookback). The value impact is tiny but must match the confirmation.
  • Live trades are partly fixed. For the current period, compound the SOFR already published, then forward to the period end. Past periods are already paid and have dropped out.
  • Clearing. Most swaps are cleared at LCH or CME. They are margined daily with price alignment interest (PAI) and discounted on OIS.
  • Reading the trade description. 9Y USD P SOFRCMP-1D-0.0415 %/R OIS-1D 16/Dec/35 reads as a 9-year USD swap maturing 16 Dec 2035. The side P is the leg you pay, here SOFR compounded daily (SOFRCMP-1D) with a spread figure attached. R is the leg you receive. Check the trade's leg details for the exact rate and spread fields before pricing.
  • Swap spread. Swap rate minus the government bond yield of the same maturity. Traders use it to compare rates and bond markets. It can be negative (USD 30Y has been for years).

Worked example

We value at the start date, 25 Nov 2026, a 5-year USD SOFR OIS. You pay 3.40% fixed on $50m and receive compounded SOFR. Both legs are annual and ACT/360. Payment dates are 25 Nov each year (we ignore weekend adjustment and the 2-day payment lag). Zero rates (continuous, ACT/365) for the 1 to 5 year dates are 3.55%, 3.45%, 3.45%, 3.50% and 3.55%.

  1. Accruals and discount factors. 2028 has 366 days.
    τ = 1.01389, 1.01667, 1.01389, 1.01389, 1.01389 D = 0.965123, 0.933238, 0.901591, 0.869275, 0.837280
  2. Annuity.
    A = Σ τ·D = 0.97853 + 0.94879 + 0.91412 + 0.88135 + 0.84891 = 4.571689
  3. Floating leg by telescoping (the forwards are 3.56%, 3.36%, 3.46%, 3.67%, 3.77%).
    PV_float / N = 1 − 0.837280 = 0.162720
  4. Par swap rate.
    S = 0.162720 / 4.571689 = 3.5593%
  5. PV of the payer swap.
    PV = 50,000,000 × (0.035593 − 0.034000) × 4.571689 = $364,150
  6. DV01 by shifting every zero rate up 1bp and repricing.
    DV01 = $23,216 (shortcut N × A × 0.0001 = $22,858)

The payer swap is worth +$364,150, because the market rate (3.56%) is 15.9bp above the 3.40% you pay. DV01 is about $23k per bp.

Remember

  • A swap exchanges fixed for floating interest on a notional that never changes hands.
  • Annuity A = Σ accrual × discount factor. Fixed leg = N × K × A.
  • With one OIS curve, floating leg = N × (Dstart − Dend), so par rate = (Dstart − Dend) / A.
  • PVpayer = N × (par − fixed) × A. DV01 ≈ N × A × 1bp.
  • IBOR legs need a projection curve for forwards and OIS for discounting.
