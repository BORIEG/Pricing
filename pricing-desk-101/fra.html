Forward rate agreements

A bet on one future interest rate fixing, settled with a single payment. It is the smallest building block of every swap, so it is where the swaps part of the course starts.

Level 1 The idea

Your family plans to borrow money in June next year for three months. The bank will charge whatever the 3-month interest rate is on that day. You are worried rates will go up before then.

An FRA lets you lock the rate today. You agree a rate now (say 2.3575%). In June you look at the real 3-month rate. If it came out higher than your locked rate, the other side pays you the difference. If it came out lower, you pay them. Either way, your borrowing ends up costing you the locked rate.

Think of pre-ordering a concert ticket at a fixed price. If tickets later sell for more, you saved money. If they sell for less, you overpaid. Nobody exchanges the ticket itself in an FRA: you only settle the price difference.

No loan is actually made. The notional (here SEK 100 million) is only used to size the payment.

Level 2 The cashflows

Four dates matter: today (trade), the fixing date (when the floating rate is observed, usually two business days before the start), the start T1, and the end T2 of the interest period.

Think of it as two promised payments at T2. The floating leg is the interest at the rate that fixes in June. The fixed leg is the interest at the rate agreed today. Only the difference changes hands. The buyer of the FRA pays fixed and receives floating.

Net payment to the FRA buyer, if paid at the end N × (L − K) × τ
N
notional (SEK 100,000,000)
L
the floating rate that fixes for the period (3M STIBOR)
K
the fixed rate agreed today (2.3575%)
τ
year fraction of the period: days ÷ 360 for STIBOR (ACT/360); 92 days gives 0.2556

Level 3 The PV formula

We don't know L today, but the market tells us what it expects it to be: the forward rate F. You read it off the projection curve's discount factors P(t), as covered in the toolkit:

Step 1 · forward rate from the projection (STIBOR) curve F = 1τ ( Pproj(T1)Pproj(T2) − 1 )

Replace the unknown L by F, then bring the payment back to today with the discount curve (the OIS curve for collateralised trades):

Step 2 · present value for the buyer (pay fixed, receive floating) PV = N × τ × (F − K) × D(T2)
Pproj(t)
projection-curve discount factor, used only to compute forwards
D(t)
discounting-curve factor (SEK OIS for a cleared or CSA trade)

Replacing an unknown future rate by its forward is legitimate for linear payoffs, where the payment moves one-for-one with the rate. That single trick prices FRAs, swaps and forwards. It stops working for options, which is why they need a volatility.

Level 4 Desk reality

  • Paid at the start, discounted. Real FRAs settle at T1, not T2. The amount is the end payment discounted at the fixing itself: N(L − K)τ ÷ (1 + Lτ). Its PV is almost identical to Step 2. The tiny difference is a convexity effect that desks ignore on a 3-month FRA.
  • Two curves. Forwards come from the STIBOR (or EURIBOR, SOFR term) curve and discounting from OIS. Using one curve for both is the classic pre-2008 shortcut and is wrong today.
  • Reading the trade description. FRA SEK|STIBOR|3M|2.357500 %|16/Jun/27 means currency, index, tenor, fixed rate and start date. The end date is start + 3M, adjusted to a business day (modified following).
  • FRA vs STIR future. A 3M STIR future (next part of the course) is the exchange-traded cousin. It is margined daily, which creates a small convexity adjustment: future-implied rate > FRA rate.

Worked example

We price an example trade on 23 Nov 2026. The trade is a buyer of SEK 100m 3M STIBOR FRA at K = 2.3575%, starting 16 Jun 2027 (205 days away) and ending 16 Sep 2027 (297 days, so 92 days long). For simplicity both curves are flat: STIBOR zero rate 2.40% and SEK OIS zero rate 2.25%, both continuously compounded ACT/365.

  1. Year fractions. Curve time uses ACT/365, the accrual uses ACT/360.
    T1 = 205/365 = 0.56164 T2 = 297/365 = 0.81370 τ = 92/360 = 0.25556
  2. Projection discount factors P = e−zt.
    P(T1) = e^(−0.024×0.56164) = 0.986611 P(T2) = e^(−0.024×0.81370) = 0.980661
  3. Forward rate.
    F = (0.986611 / 0.980661 − 1) / 0.25556 = 0.0060676 / 0.25556 = 2.3743%
    It is slightly below 2.40% because a continuously compounded ACT/365 rate is not quoted the same way as a simple ACT/360 rate.
  4. Discount factor to the end date from OIS.
    D(T2) = e^(−0.0225×0.81370) = 0.981858
  5. PV.
    PV = 100,000,000 × 0.25556 × (0.023743 − 0.023575) × 0.981858 = SEK 4,215
  6. DV01.
    DV01 ≈ 100,000,000 × 0.25556 × 0.981858 × 0.0001 = SEK 2,509 per bp

The buyer's FRA is worth +SEK 4,215. The market's forward (2.374%) is 1.7bp above the locked 2.3575%, and 1.7 × 2,509 ≈ 4,215.

Remember

  • An FRA is one fixed-vs-floating exchange on one future period, netted into one payment.
  • PV = notional × accrual × (forward − strike) × discount factor.
  • Forwards come from the projection curve. Discounting comes from OIS.
  • Its risk is one DV01 bucket and almost no gamma. A swap is simply a strip of these. See interest rate swaps.
